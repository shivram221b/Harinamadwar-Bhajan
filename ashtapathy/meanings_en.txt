@@ 0.1 | Dhyāna Ślokams |
Summary
These three meditation verses are recited before beginning the Gīta Govindam. In the first, we pray that the dark-blue radiance called "Kṛṣṇa" — the adornment of the gopīs, the joy of Lakṣmī and the eye-opening salve of the yogis — may shine forth in our hearts. In the next two, we bow to the poet Jayadeva, king of poets, beloved of Padmāvatī, who sang the nectar of Rādhā and Mādhava's divine play.

Upanyasam take-away
There is a deliberate order here: first the Lord, then the ācārya who experienced Him, and only then the Lord's story from that ācārya's words. The tongue that sings of Kṛṣṇa must first bow to the guru.
The closing words of the first verse, "hṛdaye āvirastu" (may He appear in the heart), are significant. The prayer is not "may I sing well" but "Kṛṣṇa, first appear in my heart; only then will You appear in my words." The same Lord is beauty to the gopī, bliss to Lakṣmī and wisdom to the yogi; He reveals Himself to each devotee in the mood in which that devotee approaches Him.

Detailed meaning — verse by verse

1. yadgOpI vadanEndu maNDanam abhUt kastUrikA patrakam…
Meaning: That which became the musk ornament on the moon-like face of the gopī; that which blossomed as a blue lotus on the golden-pot-like bosom of Lakṣmī; that which serves as the perfected eye-salve for yogis striving for liberation — may that dark radiance called "Kṛṣṇa" manifest in our hearts.
Key message: Rather than seeking Kṛṣṇa outside, we pray that He shine within the heart. That is the devotee's first prayer.

2. rAdhA manOrama ramAvara rAsa lIlA…
Meaning: I always bow to Jayadeva, king among poets, who alone sang as nectar the rāsa play of Lakṣmī's Lord that delights Rādhā's heart; who is a home of loving devotion in the worship of Śrī Mādhava; and who is most dear to Padmāvatī.
Key message: Before speaking of the Lord, we honour the ācārya who experienced Him. For Jayadeva, singing Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa's play was not literature but drinking nectar.

3. shrI gOpAla vilAsinI valaya sadratnAti mugdhAkrti…
Meaning: I worship the true guru Jayadeva, beloved of Padmāvatī — whose heart was captivated by Gopāla's divine play, who is ever immersed in the ocean of bliss of worshipping the lotus feet of Rādhā's Lord, who is renowned in the world as king among true poets, and who is an ocean of compassion.
Key message: The first verse says Kṛṣṇa must appear in the heart; the third says we must then be immersed in the ocean of devotion. This is Jayadeva's invitation at the very doorway of the Gīta Govindam.

@@ 1.1 | Ślokam — Opening of the Gīta Govindam |
Summary
These four ślokams open the first sarga and form the gateway to the Gīta Govindam. Night has fallen and Nanda sends Rādhā to take Kṛṣṇa home; on the way, on the banks of the Yamunā, their divine play begins. Jayadeva then introduces himself, praises the great poets of his time, and finally invites us: "If your mind delights in remembering Hari, listen to these songs."

Upanyasam take-away
One does not need Sanskrit scholarship or śāstric learning to enjoy the Gīta Govindam. Jayadeva asks only one thing: "yadi harismaraṇe sarasaṃ manaḥ" — does your mind find delight in remembering Hari? That is enough.
Three people hear the same song. To one it is a beautiful love poem; to another, the soul's longing for the Supreme; to a third, the highest state of divine love. The song is the same; the listener's heart decides the experience.
In one line: "Once the mind finds delight in remembering Hari, joy in hearing Hari's play comes by itself."

Detailed meaning — verse by verse

1. mEghairmEduram ambaram vanabhuva: shyAmAstamAladrumai:…
Meaning: The sky is thick with clouds, the forest is dark with tamāla trees, and night has come. Nanda says, "This child is afraid of the dark; Rādhā, you take him home." The two set out as told, and every bower and tree along the Yamunā's bank becomes witness to their secret divine play. May those plays of Rādhā and Mādhava be victorious.
Key message: We plan one thing; the Lord brings about another. Nanda wanted the child to reach home; the Lord willed that Rādhā and Mādhava should meet. That is what "līlā" means.

2. vAgdEvatA carita citrita cittasadmA…
Meaning: Jayadeva, whose mind is adorned by the grace of Sarasvatī, the goddess of speech, and who is foremost in singing at the feet of Padmāvatī, composes this work containing the loving play of Śrī Vāsudeva.
Key message: Jayadeva does not say "I composed this"; he says Sarasvatī adorned his mind. A true devotee-artist feels that grace has flowed through him.

3. yadi harismaraNE sarasam mana:…
Meaning: If your mind delights in remembering Hari, and if you are curious about the art of His divine play, then listen to this speech of Jayadeva — sweet, tender and lovely in its words.
Key message: An ear that hears is not enough; a mind that can relish ("sarasaṃ manaḥ") is the first qualification for the Gīta Govindam.

4. vAca: pallavayati umApatidhara:…
Meaning: Umāpatidhara makes words blossom; Śaraṇa is praised for swiftly composing difficult verse; none rivals Ācārya Govardhana in composing the finest themes of śṛṅgāra; the poet-king Dhoyī is famed for his memory. But Jayadeva alone knows the purity of composition. Thus, praising each poet of his time, he indicates that he offers a work combining all their excellences.
Key message: A great person loses nothing by acknowledging the greatness of others. That is true magnanimity.

@@ 1.2 | Aṣṭapadi 1 — Pralaya Payodhijale (Daśāvatāra song) |
Summary
This is the hymn to the ten avatāras. Each stanza praises one incarnation — Matsya, Kūrma, Varāha, Narasiṃha, Vāmana, Paraśurāma, Śrī Rāma, Balarāma, Buddha and Kalki — and ends with "Keśava, who took this form, Lord of the world, Hari, victory to You!" The last stanza is the phalaśruti: this song gives happiness and auspiciousness and reveals the essence of life.

Upanyasam take-away
This should not be seen as a mere list of ten avatāras. One Supreme Being takes different forms according to the time and the need, and protects dharma. He protects knowledge (Matsya), supports unseen from behind (Kūrma), lifts the fallen (Varāha), and appears wherever the devotee calls (Narasiṃha).
Why does Jayadeva sing the Daśāvatāra before the play of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa? To establish at the outset that the Kṛṣṇa of this poem is no ordinary lover, but the Supreme Lord who took all ten avatāras.

Detailed meaning — stanza by stanza

1. pralaya payOdhijalE dhrtavAnasi vEdam… (Matsya)
Meaning: In the waters of the cosmic deluge You held up the Vedas so they would not perish, tirelessly carrying them like a boat. Keśava, who took the form of a fish, Lord of the world, Hari — victory to You!
Key message: Protecting the world is not enough; the knowledge that guides the world must also be protected. That is why the first avatāra saves the Vedas.

2. kSitirati vipulatarE tava tiSThati prSThE… (Kūrma)
Meaning: The vast earth rests on Your broad back, which bears the scars of supporting it. During the churning of the ocean You held up Mount Mandara as its base. Keśava, who took the form of a tortoise — victory to You!
Key message: The gods and demons churned, but the Lord supported from beneath. Often in life He does not come to the front; He holds us up from behind. That too is His grace.

3. vasati dashana shikharE dharaNI tava lagnA… (Varāha)
Meaning: Like the mark on the moon, the Earth, sunk in the deluge, rests clinging to the tip of Your tusk as You lift her up. Keśava, who took the form of a boar — victory to You!
Key message: In the Lord's eyes no one is "fallen"; there are only those waiting to be lifted up.

4. tavakarakamalavarE nakhamadbhuta shrngam… (Narasiṃha)
Meaning: On Your excellent lotus-hand the wondrous nails are like sharp horns, with which You tore apart the body of Hiraṇyakaśipu, who was like a bee. Keśava, who took the form of the man-lion — victory to You!
Key message: Hiraṇyakaśipu's boon forbade death by man or beast, by day or night, inside or outside. The Lord came as neither man nor lion, at twilight, on the threshold. However well adharma fortifies itself, the Lord finds a way. "Wherever I call, He will come" — that was Prahlāda's faith.

5. chalayasi vikramaNE balimadbhuta vAmana… (Vāmana)
Meaning: Wondrous dwarf! With Your mighty strides You outwitted Mahābali, and the sacred water (the Gaṅgā) flowing from Your toenail purifies the people. Keśava, who took the form of Vāmana — victory to You!
Key message: Do not measure power by size. Vāmana was small, yet measured the three worlds in three steps.

6. kSatriyarudhiramayE jagadapagata pApam… (Paraśurāma)
Meaning: Destroying the kṣatriyas sunk in adharma, You bathed the world in their blood, washing away its sin and relieving the suffering of worldly existence. Keśava, who took the form of the Lord of the Bhṛgus — victory to You!
Key message: This is not about destroying a community. Power must serve dharma; when power turns against dharma, the Lord restrains it.

7. vitarasi dikSuraNE dikpatikamanIyam… (Śrī Rāma)
Meaning: In battle You offered the heads of ten-headed Rāvaṇa as a pleasing oblation to the guardians of the directions. Keśava, who took the form of Rāma — victory to You!
Key message: Speaking about dharma is one thing; living it is another. "Rāmo vigrahavān dharmaḥ" — Rāma is dharma embodied.

8. vahasi vapuSi vishadE vasanan jaladAbham… (Balarāma)
Meaning: On Your fair body You wear a cloud-blue garment, coloured like the Yamunā, who came to You in fear of the blow of Your plough. Keśava, who took the form of the plough-bearer — victory to You!
Key message: As the plough prepares the soil, a hardened mind must first be softened; only then will the seed of devotion sprout.

9. nindasi yajnavidhE: ahaha shrutijAtam… (Buddha)
Meaning: With a compassionate heart You censured the Vedic injunctions that prescribed animal slaughter in sacrifice. Keśava, who took the form of Buddha — victory to You!
Key message: Compassion is the heart of dharma. Ritual without compassion becomes mere formality.

10. mlEccha nivaha nidhanE kalayasi karavAlam… (Kalki)
Meaning: To destroy the multitude of the unrighteous You wield a sword, terrifying like a comet. Keśava, who took the form of Kalki — victory to You!
Key message: However much adharma grows, it is not permanent. In the end, dharma prevails.

11. shrI jayadEvakavE: idamuditam udAram… (Phalaśruti)
Meaning: Listen to this noble song spoken by the poet Śrī Jayadeva. It gives happiness and auspiciousness and conveys the essence of existence. Keśava, who took ten forms, Lord of the world, Hari — victory to You!
Key message: The same Keśava shines in all ten avatāras; singing of Him is the essence of life.

@@ 2.1 | Ślokam — Summary of the ten avatāras |
Summary
In this single verse Jayadeva sums up the ten avatāras sung in the first aṣṭapadi and concludes: "Salutations to You, Kṛṣṇa, who took these ten forms."

Upanyasam take-away
"Daśākṛtikṛte kṛṣṇāya tubhyaṃ namaḥ" is the heart of this verse. The ten avatāras are not ten gods but ten manifestations of one Supreme Being. When the Veda is lost — Matsya; when support is needed — Kūrma; when the earth falls — Varāha; when the devotee calls — Narasiṃha; when pride grows — Vāmana; when power becomes unjust — Paraśurāma; to live dharma — Rāma; to set the foundation right — Balarāma; when compassion fades — Buddha; when adharma crosses all limits — Kalki.
The forms are ten, the times are ten, the needs are ten — but the Lord is one, and His purpose is one: to establish dharma and protect the devotee.

Detailed meaning

1. vEdAnuddharatE jagannivahatE bhUgOlam udbibhratE…
Meaning: To You who rescued the Vedas (Matsya), bore the world (Kūrma), lifted the globe of the earth (Varāha), tore apart the demon (Narasiṃha), outwitted Bali (Vāmana), destroyed the kṣatriyas (Paraśurāma), conquered Rāvaṇa of Pulastya's line (Rāma), wielded the plough (Balarāma), spread compassion (Buddha), and will strike down the unrighteous (Kalki) — to You, Kṛṣṇa, who took these ten forms, my salutations.
Key message: That is why the refrain "Keśava, who took this form" is repeated after each avatāra: the form changes, but the salutation always reaches the same Keśava.

@@ 2.2 | Aṣṭapadi 2 — Śrita Kamalākuca Maṇḍala |
Summary
In the first aṣṭapadi we saw who the Lord is through His ten avatāras. Here Jayadeva describes the beauty of His form, His auspicious qualities and His deeds one by one, ending each stanza with "Victory, victory to the Lord Hari." We see Him as the consort of Lakṣmī, adorned with earrings and a forest garland, the ornament of the sun's orb, the subduer of Kāliya, and as Śrī Rāma, the ornament of Sītā.

Upanyasam take-away
The very first line states a great truth: "śrita kamalākuca maṇḍala" — He who rests on the bosom of Lakṣmī. The Lord and the Goddess are inseparable; Śrīman Nārāyaṇa is always with Śrī.
Another beauty: His eyes are soft as lotus petals (amala kamaladala locana), yet the same gaze cuts the bonds of worldly existence (bhavamocana). Gentleness and power together — that is the Lord's compassion. Singing His qualities is not only music to the ear; it is auspiciousness for the heart.

Detailed meaning — stanza by stanza

1. shrita kamalAkuca maNDala dhrtakuNDala…
Meaning: You who rest on the bosom of Lakṣmī, who wear beautiful earrings and a charming forest garland — victory, victory to You, Lord Hari!
Key message: Where Lakṣmī is, Nārāyaṇa must also be; prosperity and devotion should go together.

2. dinamaNi maNDala maNDana bhavakhaNDana…
Meaning: Ornament of the sun's orb, destroyer of worldly bondage, swan dwelling in the lake of the sages' minds — victory, victory to You, Lord Hari!
Key message: As the swan separates milk from water, the wise live in the world yet take only its essence. In such minds the Lord dwells. Wisdom is not abandoning the world but discerning the essential from the inessential.

3. kAliya viSadhara bhanjana janaranjana…
Meaning: Subduer of the venomous serpent Kāliya, delighter of the people, sun to the lotus of the Yadu clan — victory, victory to You, Lord Hari!
Key message: Kāliya poisoned the Yamunā, as the poison in our minds poisons what is around us. Kṛṣṇa did not kill Kāliya; He danced on his heads and subdued him. The Lord's feet must first rest upon the poison within us.

4. madhumura naraka vinAshana garuDAsana…
Meaning: Slayer of Madhu, Mura and Naraka, rider on Garuḍa, cause of the joyful play of the gods — victory, victory to You, Lord Hari!
Key message: Where the Lord appears, fear departs: joy for the good, an end for adharma.

5. amala kamaladala lOcana bhavamOcana…
Meaning: You whose eyes are like spotless lotus petals, who release beings from worldly existence, who are the support of the three worlds — victory, victory to You, Lord Hari!
Key message: The Lord's compassion is gentle, yet strong enough to cut our deepest bonds.

6. janakasutA kucabhUSaNa jitadUSaNa…
Meaning: Ornament of Sītā, daughter of Janaka; conqueror of Dūṣaṇa; who ended the ten-headed Rāvaṇa in battle — victory, victory to You, Lord Hari!
Key message: Rāma's greatness lies not only in defeating Rāvaṇa but in upholding dharma as Sītā's husband. Upholding dharma in relationships is also heroism.

7. abhinava jaladhara sundara dhrtamandara…
Meaning: Beautiful as a fresh rain cloud, bearer of Mount Mandara, cakora bird delighting in the moon-like face of Śrī — victory, victory to You, Lord Hari!
Key message: As the cakora lives on moonlight, the sight of the Lord's face is the devotee's nourishment.

8. shrI jayadEva kavEridan kurutE mudam…
Meaning: This radiant, auspicious song of the poet Śrī Jayadeva brings joy to those who hear it. Victory, victory to You, Lord Hari!
Key message: Singing the Lord's qualities is music to the ear and auspiciousness to the heart.

@@ 2.3 | Ślokam |
Summary
The verse describes Madhusūdana's chest, marked with saffron from embracing Lakṣmī, and blesses the listeners that this beloved form may bring them all that is dear and auspicious.

Upanyasam take-away
Jayadeva sees the saffron mark on the Lord's chest not as mere decoration but as "vyaktānurāgam iva" — love made visible. Love held within leaves its mark outside. So too devotion: it does not stay only in the heart; its signs appear in one's life.

Detailed meaning

1. padmApayOdhara taTI parirambhalagna-…
Meaning: The chest of Madhusūdana, marked with the Kashmir saffron of Padmā's bosom from His embrace, appears like love made visible, and is further bathed in drops of perspiration from the fatigue of play. May it fully grant you all that is dear to you.
Key message: Even on the Lord's own form, love appears as a sign; that sign of love is our blessing.

@@ 3.1 | Ślokam |
Summary
It is spring. Rādhā, whose limbs are as tender as spring flowers, wanders through the forest searching everywhere for Kṛṣṇa. Seeing her distressed by the fever of love, her companion speaks to her gently.

Upanyasam take-away
Here the Gīta Govindam takes a major turn. Until now we looked at Kṛṣṇa directly; from here we search for Him through Rādhā's eyes. He is near, yet she does not have His presence — this is viraha bhakti, devotion in separation.
Spring is joy for everyone, but for Rādhā it is pain. For the devotee, however beautiful the world may be, it is incomplete without the Lord.

Detailed meaning

1. vasantE vAsantIkusuma sukumArai: avayavai:…
Meaning: In spring, Rādhā, with limbs as delicate as vāsantī flowers, wanders in the forest following Kṛṣṇa in many places. Seeing her deeply troubled by the anxiety born of love's fever, her companion spoke these tender words.
Key message: Even if the whole world is in spring, without Kṛṣṇa in the heart it is no spring for the devotee.

@@ 3.2 | Aṣṭapadi 3 — Lalita Lavaṅga Latā |
Summary
The companion describes Vṛndāvana in spring: the Malaya breeze blows, bees hum, cuckoos call and flowers bloom everywhere. "In this delightful spring, Hari is here dancing with young women — but, friend, for those in separation this season is unbearable." In each stanza a new beauty of nature deepens Rādhā's longing.

Upanyasam take-away
There is a subtle truth here. The breeze is the same, the cuckoo the same, the flowers the same; but because Rādhā's state of mind has changed, the same spring becomes painful. Happiness and sorrow do not come from outside; our inner state gives them their colour.
Yet in the last stanza Jayadeva states the essence: "haricaraṇa smṛti sāram" — however much nature is described, its purpose is to remind us of Hari's feet.

Detailed meaning — stanza by stanza

1. lalita lavanga latA parishIlana kOmala malaya samIrE…
Meaning: The soft Malaya breeze caresses the lovely clove creepers; the bowers are filled with swarms of bees and the calls of cuckoos. In this delightful spring Hari roams here, friend, dancing with young women in a season that is cruel to those in separation.
Key message: What happens outside is not greater than our state of mind; the world appears as the mind is.

2. unmada madana manOrathapathika vadhUjana janita vilApE…
Meaning: Women whose husbands are away lament, stirred by intoxicating desire; the bakula trees bloom in clusters crowded with bees. (In such a spring Hari roams.)
Key message: Spring delights those who are together and pains those who are apart; it magnifies what is already in the heart.

3. mrgamada saurabha rabhasa vashamvada navadala mAla tamAlE…
Meaning: The tamāla trees with fresh leaves give off a fragrance like musk, and the red kiṃśuka blossoms look like the nails of Love that tear the hearts of the young.
Key message: Whatever we dwell upon, everything we see reminds us of it.

4. madana mahIpati kanaka daNTaruci kEsara kusumavikAsE…
Meaning: The kesara flowers bloom golden like the sceptre of King Love; the pāṭala blossoms crowded with bees look like Love's quiver.
Key message: In spring, nature itself becomes Love's armoury.

5. vigalita lajjita jagadavalOkana taruNa karuNa krtahAsE…
Meaning: Flowers bloom everywhere as if smiling at a world that has lost its shyness, while ketakī blossoms, like spear-tips that pierce those in separation, fill the directions.
Key message: A sight that is beautiful to those together becomes a weapon to those apart. To a heart separated from the Lord, even the world's beauty wounds.

6. mAdhavikA parimala militE nava mAlikayAti sugandhau…
Meaning: The fragrance of mādhavī flowers mingles with the rich scent of fresh jasmine. This spring can bewitch even the minds of sages and is a friend without reason to the young.
Key message: "It bewitches even sages" — even one who thinks he has conquered the senses must remain watchful; the mind is very subtle.

7. sphuradati mukta latA parirambhaNa mukulita pulakita cUtE…
Meaning: Mango trees, embraced by atimukta creepers, seem to bud and thrill. In the forest of Vṛndāvana, purified by the surrounding waters of the Yamunā, Hari roams.
Key message: When union is seen all around, the loneliness of one in separation grows deeper.

8. shrI jayadEva bhaNitamidam udayati haricaraNasmrtisAram…
Meaning: May this utterance of Śrī Jayadeva shine forth — its essence the remembrance of Hari's feet, describing the forest in delightful spring and the feelings of love it awakens.
Key message: Even while enjoying nature, its final purpose should be to remind us of Hari's feet.

@@ 3.3 | Ślokam |
Summary
Two verses. In the first, the spring breeze carrying jasmine pollen and ketakī fragrance burns the heart like the very life-breath of Love. In the second, travellers far from home pass these days of mango buds and cuckoo calls only by meditating on the moment of reunion with their beloved.

Upanyasam take-away
A scent, a breeze, a melody — any of these can revive an entire past experience. For one in separation a single day feels like an age, yet the hope of reunion sustains life. Separation without hope is suffering; separation with hope becomes devotion. Meditating on union with the Lord is the devotee's life-breath.

Detailed meaning — verse by verse

1. daravidalita malli vallI cancat parAga…
Meaning: The pollen stirring from half-opened jasmine creepers scents the forest like perfumed powder. This breeze, friend of the ketakī's fragrance, spreads like the life-breath of Love and burns the heart here.
Key message: Memories do not come only through words; even a fragrance can revive a past experience completely.

2. unmIlan madhugandhalubdha madhupa vyAdhUta cUtAnkura-…
Meaning: Bees greedy for honey shake the mango buds, and the sweet calls of playing cuckoos bring a fever to the ears. Travellers somehow pass these days only through the joy of a moment's union, in meditation, with the one as dear as their life.
Key message: Separation sustained by the hope of reunion blossoms into devotion.

@@ 4.1 | Ślokam |
Summary
The companion points out to Rādhā, from close by, Murāri eagerly embracing many women and absorbed in charming play, and speaks to her again.

Upanyasam take-away
Until now Rādhā searched for Kṛṣṇa. Now her friend says, "Look, there He is!" — but He is not with Rādhā; He is with the other gopīs. "Kṛṣṇa is not with me" is painful; "He is with others and not with me" is more painful still.
This should not be read as worldly jealousy but as the high devotional state of the soul longing to experience the Lord as its very own.

Detailed meaning

1. anEkanArI parirambha sambhrama…
Meaning: Showing Rādhā, from nearby, Murāri, who was eager in the embraces of many women and longing for charming play, the companion spoke to her again.
Key message: Even when the Lord is near, the feeling that He is not "mine" intensifies the pain of separation.

@@ 4.2 | Aṣṭapadi 4 — Candanacarcita Nīlakalebara |
Summary
The companion describes Kṛṣṇa's play in Vṛndāvana: His dark body smeared with sandal, yellow garment, forest garland, swinging jewelled earrings and smiling face — Hari sports amid a crowd of charming young gopīs. One embraces Him and sings in the high fifth note; one meditates on His lotus face; one whispers in His ear; one pulls Him by His garment; one dances with Him in the rāsa and is praised. He delights each one individually.

Upanyasam take-away
This śṛṅgāra description should not be seen as worldly romance alone; in the Vaiṣṇava tradition it is enjoyed as the highest state of love between the soul and the Supreme.
The seventh stanza is its heart: He embraces one, kisses another, delights another, looks at another with a smile, follows another. Each gopī feels "Kṛṣṇa is mine alone." The Lord's love does not diminish when shared; each devotee receives Him in full.
When true devotion fills the heart it finds expression — as song, meditation, service or dance.

Detailed meaning — stanza by stanza

1. candanacarcita nIlakalEbara pItavasana vanamAlI…
Meaning: His dark-blue body is smeared with sandal; He wears a yellow garment and a forest garland; His cheeks are adorned with jewelled earrings swinging in play, and He is radiant with a smile. That Hari sports here amid a crowd of charming young women eager for play.
Key message: The Lord's beauty is not only to attract the eye but to draw the mind to Him.

2. pInapayOdhara bhArabharENa harim parirabhya sarAgam…
Meaning: One cowherd woman lovingly embraces Hari and sings along with Him in the raised fifth note.
Key message: When love fills the heart, it overflows as melody and kīrtana.

3. kA'pi vilAsa vilOla vilOcana khElana janita manOjam…
Meaning: Another, enchanted by the love awakened by the playful movement of His eyes, deeply meditates on the lotus face of Madhusūdana.
Key message: A single glance of the Lord can take over the devotee's entire mind.

4. kA'pi kapOlatalE militA lapitum kimapi shrutimUlE…
Meaning: Another, drawing close to His cheek as if to whisper something in His ear, thrilled with joy, tenderly kisses her beloved.
Key message: Some prayers are not for a crowd; they are the heart's secret shared only between the devotee and the Lord.

5. kElikalA kutukEna ca kAcit amum yamunAvanakUlE…
Meaning: Another, eager for play, pulls Him by His garment towards her as He stands in a lovely vañjula bower on the Yamunā bank.
Key message: When love becomes free of fear, the relationship between devotee and Lord becomes very intimate.

6. karatalatAla tarala valayAvali kalita kalasvanavamshE…
Meaning: Amid the clapping of hands, the jingling of bangles and the sweet sound of the flute, Hari praised a young woman who danced with Him in the joy of the rāsa.
Key message: At the height of devotion, "I am singing" gives way to "I sing together with the Lord."

7. shliSyati kAmapi cumbati kAmapi kAmapi ramayati rAmAm…
Meaning: He embraces one, kisses another, delights another, gazes at a smiling beauty, and follows yet another.
Key message: The Lord's love does not diminish when shared; He belongs fully to every devotee.

8. shrI jayadEva bhaNitamidam adbhuta kEshava kElirahasyam…
Meaning: May this wondrous secret of Keśava's play in the Vṛndāvana forest, sung by Śrī Jayadeva, spread auspiciousness and glory to all.
Key message: Understanding the rāsa is not watching an outer event but realising the divine love within it.

@@ 4.3 | Ślokam — Conclusion of the first sarga |
Summary
The first sarga ends with three verses. In the first, Hari plays in spring like śṛṅgāra (love) itself in bodily form. In the second, the Malaya breeze and the "kuhū kuhū" of cuckoos fill the spring. In the third, amid the rāsa, Rādhā, blind with love, embraces Kṛṣṇa and praises His face as nectar, and He kisses her with a smile; may that Hari protect us. Thus ends the first sarga, "Sāmoda Dāmodara".

Upanyasam take-away
"Śṛṅgāraḥ mūrtimān iva" — Kṛṣṇa is not merely the hero of a love story; He is love itself: its source, its form and its goal.
Notice the turn: in the third aṣṭapadi Rādhā searched; in the fourth He was with others; now Rādhā reaches Him. Even amid many devotees, the Lord seeks out His intimate devotee.

Detailed meaning — verse by verse

1. vishvESAm anuranjanEna janayan AnandamindIvara-…
Meaning: Friend, delighting all and creating joy, celebrating a festival of love with limbs dark and soft as a row of blue lotuses, embraced limb by limb on all sides by the beauties of Vraja, charming Hari plays freely in spring like śṛṅgāra in bodily form.
Key message: Kṛṣṇa does not only receive love; He is its source, its form and its goal.

2. adyOtsanga vasadbhujanga kabala klEshAdiva IshAcalam…
Meaning: As if to escape the pain of being swallowed by the serpents living on its slopes, and wishing to bathe in snow, the breeze from the sandal mountains now moves towards the Himālaya. And seeing the tender buds on the tops of the mango trees, the cuckoos joyfully raise their sweet "kuhū kuhū".
Key message: Nature does not merely watch the Lord's play; it too becomes a devotee and praises Him.

3. rAsOllAsabharENa vibhramabhrtAm AbhIravAma bhrvAm…
Meaning: Near the cowherd beauties lost in the rapture of the rāsa, Rādhā, blind with love, embraced His chest tightly and, saying "Your face is full of nectar," praised Him in song. Using that praise as a pretext, He kissed her eagerly; may Hari, charming with His smile, protect you.
Key message: Even among many devotees, the Lord never forgets His intimate one.
@@ 5.1 | Ślokam — Opening of the second sarga |
Summary
The second sarga begins. Seeing Hari sport with all the gopīs with equal affection, Rādhā, hurt that her special place seems lost, goes away and hides in a bower humming with bees, and speaks sadly and secretly to her companion.

Upanyasam take-away
"The Lord belongs to everyone" is knowledge; "the Lord is closest to me" is experience. As devotion deepens, the soul moves from the first to the second. Rādhā's hurt is not ego but the intensity of a love that longs to experience the Lord as her own.

Detailed meaning

1. viharati vanE rAdhA sAdhAraNa praNayE harau…
Meaning: As Hari roamed the forest with love shared equally among all, Rādhā, jealous that her own excellence had been diminished, went elsewhere. Hiding in a bower of creepers filled with humming bees, she spoke sorrowfully and in secret to her friend.
Key message: In deep devotion the Lord is experienced not as common to all but as "mine".

@@ 5.2 | Aṣṭapadi 5 — Sañcarad Adhara Sudhā |
Summary
Rādhā tells her companion: "My mind keeps remembering Hari, who sported here in the rāsa and teased me playfully." She recalls His flute music, His hair circled with peacock feathers, His form like a rain cloud with a rainbow, the smile on His red lips, the thousands of gopīs around Him, His sandal mark and makara earrings. At the end she longs: "May He who stands beneath the kadamba tree delight me too with His glance."

Upanyasam take-away
The refrain throughout is "smarati mano mama" — my mind remembers. The play is over, but the experience does not leave the mind. That is smaraṇa. Remembering the Lord is not merely recalling His name; it is His form, qualities and deeds coming alive in the mind.
The seventh stanza is the heart: "Let Him belong to everyone, but may He also bless me personally." The Lord is common to all, yet in the devotee's heart He becomes uniquely one's own.

Detailed meaning — stanza by stanza

1. sancarad adhara sudhA madhuradhvani mukharita mOhana vamsham…
Meaning: He fills His enchanting flute with sweet sound like nectar flowing from His lips; His glances, head, cheeks and swinging earrings sway as He plays. My mind remembers Hari, who sported here in the rāsa and teased me playfully.
Key message: In true remembrance, the Lord's form and deeds come alive in the mind.

2. candraka cAru mayUra shikhaNDaka maNDala valayita kEsham…
Meaning: His hair is encircled by lovely peacock feathers with their eye-like markings; His appearance is like a dense rain cloud coloured by Indra's bow (the rainbow). My mind remembers Him.
Key message: Even comparing Him with the most beautiful things in nature cannot fully describe Kṛṣṇa's beauty.

3. gOpakadamba nitambavatI mukha cumbana lambita lObham…
Meaning: He longs to kiss the faces of the gopīs; His sweet lips, red as the bandhujīva flower, shine with a gentle smile. My mind remembers Him.
Key message: Devotion is not one-sided; if we take one step towards Him, He takes many towards us.

4. vipula pulaka bhuja pallava valayita vallava yuvati sahasram…
Meaning: With tender arms thrilling with joy He embraces thousands of cowherd maidens; the light of the jewels on His hands, feet and chest dispels the darkness. My mind remembers Him.
Key message: Where Kṛṣṇa is, darkness departs — both outer and inner.

5. jaladapaTala caladindu vinindaka candana bindu lalATam…
Meaning: His forehead bears a sandal mark that outshines the moon moving among clouds; His heart opens to the embrace of the gopīs. My mind remembers Him.
Key message: The Lord's heart is always open to the devotee's love.

6. maNimaya makara manOhara kuNDala maNDita gaNDamudAram…
Meaning: His noble cheeks are adorned with lovely jewelled makara earrings; He wears yellow silk and is surrounded and revered by sages, humans, gods and demons alike. My mind remembers Him.
Key message: The Lord makes no distinctions; He is the one Supreme Self for all.

7. vishada kadambatalE militam kalikaluSabhayam shamayantam…
Meaning: He stands beneath the beautiful kadamba tree, removing the fear of the sins of the Kali age, and with waves of loving glances and His form He delights even me. My mind remembers Him.
Key message: The Lord belongs to all, yet in the devotee's heart He becomes uniquely one's own.

8. shrI jayadEva bhaNitam atisundara mOhana madhuripu rUpam…
Meaning: May this most beautiful and enchanting form of Madhuripu, described by Śrī Jayadeva, be fit for the virtuous who now remember the feet of Hari.
Key message: Remembering Kṛṣṇa's form is itself meritorious; that remembrance leads us to His feet.

@@ 6.1 | Ślokam |
Summary
Rādhā tells her friend of the struggle in her mind: "My mind counts only His virtues and pushes His faults far away. Even though He sports with other young women without me, this stubborn mind longs for Him again. What shall I do?"

Upanyasam take-away
There are two voices in Rādhā. The intellect says, "He has left you." The heart says, "Still, I want Him alone." This is ananya prema, undivided love. True love does not make faults vanish, but it makes the virtues appear greater than the faults. So it is with the Lord: even while asking "Why did You do this?", the devotee's mind keeps circling around Him.

Detailed meaning

1. gaNayati guNagrAmam bhrAmam bhramAdapi nEhatE…
Meaning: My mind counts the host of His virtues and does not wish to wander elsewhere even by mistake; it finds contentment in Him and casts His faults far away. Though Kṛṣṇa, thirsting for other young women, sports without me, this contrary mind still desires Him. What can I do?
Key message: In undivided love the mind does not dwell on faults; it counts only virtues.

@@ 6.2 | Aṣṭapadi 6 — Nibhṛta Nikuñja Gṛham |
Summary
Rādhā pleads with her friend: "Friend, unite me with that noble slayer of Keśī." She then recalls, stanza by stanza, a night of union with Him in a secluded bower: the shyness of the first meeting, His sweet words, the bed of tender leaves, the thrill of joy, the sound of jewelled anklets, and her complete surrender. In the tradition, this śṛṅgāra description is understood as the soul's complete union with the Supreme.

Upanyasam take-away
In the refrain Rādhā asks one thing: "ramaya mayā saha" — unite Him with me. Here the "gopī" is not just an individual woman; she is the soul that finds satisfaction in nothing but the Lord. The feeling "mamaiva" — He is mine alone — is the life of her devotion.
The signs of devotion described in the śāstras — tears, thrill, a choked voice, losing oneself — are expressed here in the language of love. The five senses — sight, sound, smell, touch and taste — all converging on the Lord is the deeper meaning of this aṣṭapadi.

Detailed meaning — stanza by stanza

1. nibhrta nikunja grham gatayA nishi rahasi nilIya vasantam…
Meaning: He who hid at night in a secluded bower, and when I came, looked about anxiously in all directions and laughed with eager love — friend, make that noble slayer of Keśī delight with me, who am filled with longing.
Key message: The one boon the soul asks for is union with the Lord.

2. prathama samAgama lajjitayA paTu cATu shatairanukUlam…
Meaning: When I was shy at our first meeting, He won me over with hundreds of sweet words; as I spoke with a gentle smile, He loosened my garment. Unite Him with me.
Key message: The Lord Himself removes the devotee's hesitation with loving words and draws her near.

3. kisalaya shayana nivEshitayA ciram urasi mamaiva shayAnam…
Meaning: After seating me on the bed of tender leaves, He lay long upon my breast alone; embracing and kissing, He drank the nectar of my lips. Unite Him with me.
Key message: "Mamaiva" — mine alone: beyond knowing the Lord belongs to all, experiencing Him as one's own.

4. alasa nimIlita lOcanayA pulakAvali lalita kapOlam…
Meaning: As my eyes closed languidly, His cheeks glowed with thrill; as my body was bathed in perspiration, He became restless with the intoxication of love. Unite Him with me.
Key message: Tears, thrill and losing oneself are the sāttvika signs of devotion.

5. kOkila kalarava kUjitayA jita manasija tantra vicAram…
Meaning: As I cooed like a cuckoo, He surpassed all the finer points of the science of love; as my flower-strewn hair came loose, He marked me with His nails. Unite Him with me.
Key message: In the fullness of love, rules give way to experience.

6. caraNa raNita maNi nUpurayA paripUrita surata vitAnam…
Meaning: With my jewelled anklets ringing, He brought our union to fulfilment; with my girdle jingling and loosening, He held my hair and kissed me. Unite Him with me.
Key message: Sound, scent, touch, sight and taste all become one in the Lord.

7. ratisukhasamaya rasAlasayA dara mukulita nayana sarOjam…
Meaning: Madhusūdana, whose heart rejoiced when I, languid at the height of joy, with lotus eyes half-closed, sank helplessly upon Him like a creeper — unite Him with me.
Key message: When the soul surrenders completely, the Lord Himself rejoices.

8. shrI jayadEva bhaNitam idam atishaya madhuripu nidhuvana shIlam…
Meaning: Śrī Jayadeva sang this most delightful account of Madhuripu's love-play, as told by the longing Rādhā to her friend. May it spread happiness and joy to all.
Key message: The soul's longing for nothing but the Lord is perfect devotion.

@@ 6.3 | Ślokam — Conclusion of the second sarga |
Summary
The second sarga, "Akleśa Keśava", ends with three verses. In the first, Rādhā rejoices seeing Govinda, surrounded by gopīs, smile shyly on seeing her. In the second, in His absence, the aśoka creepers, the breeze by the lake and the mango buds give her pain. The third is a blessing: may Keśava, who, though seeing the gopīs' charms, kept thinking inwardly of Rādhā, remove our sorrows.

Upanyasam take-away
The third verse is worth noticing: though surrounded by many gopīs, Keśava's heart remains with Rādhā. While we search for the Lord, He too is thinking of us. The very name of the sarga says it: "Akleśa Keśava" — Keśava who removes distress.

Detailed meaning — verse by verse

1. hastasrasta vilAsavamsham anrju bhrUvalli madvallavI…
Meaning: The flute slipping from His hand; the crowd of gopīs with curved brows eagerly glancing at Him from the corners of their eyes; His cheeks wet with perspiration; His face, abashed on seeing me, charming with the nectar of a smile — I see such a Govinda in the forest, surrounded by the beauties of Vraja, and I rejoice.
Key message: Even amid many devotees, the Lord's face lights up on seeing His intimate devotee.

2. durAlOka stOka stabaka navakAshOka latikA…
Meaning: Friend, the new aśoka creepers blooming in small clusters, too beautiful to look at, and the breeze from the garden by the lake pain me. Even the mango buds, lovely with the humming of wandering bees, give me no joy now.
Key message: Joy and sorrow are not in objects; the mind that experiences them gives them colour.

3. sAkUtasmitam AkulAkula galaddhammillam ullAsita…
Meaning: Having secretly seen the gopīs' meaningful smiles, loosening hair, dancing brows and graceful gestures, and having fulfilled their wish, Keśava still remained long in thought (of Rādhā). May that charming Keśava remove your sorrows.
Key message: While we seek the Lord, He too is thinking of us.

@@ 7.1 | Ślokam — Opening of the third sarga |
Summary
The third sarga begins. Kaṃsa's enemy, Kṛṣṇa, placing in His heart Rādhā — who is like a chain binding the impressions of worldly life — leaves the other maidens of Vraja. Searching for her everywhere without success, wounded by Love's arrows and filled with remorse, Mādhava sits sorrowfully in a bower on the bank of the Yamunā.

Upanyasam take-away
A major turn: until now Rādhā sought Kṛṣṇa; now Kṛṣṇa seeks Rādhā. Not only does the devotee seek the Lord — the Lord too seeks the devotee.
Another point: once true love has a real place in the heart, other attachments need not be forced away; they loosen by themselves.

Detailed meaning — verse by verse

1. kamsArirapi samsAra vAsanAbaddha shrnkhalAm…
Meaning: Even Kṛṣṇa, the enemy of Kaṃsa, placing in His heart Rādhā — the chain that binds the impressions of worldly life — gave up the other beauties of Vraja.
Key message: When true love dwells in the heart, other attachments fall away on their own.

2. itastata: tAm anusrtya rAdhikAm…
Meaning: Following Rādhā here and there, His mind wounded by the arrows of Love and filled with remorse, Mādhava sat down dejected in a bower at the edge of the Yamunā's bank.
Key message: Not only does the devotee seek the Lord; the Lord too searches for His devotee.

@@ 7.2 | Aṣṭapadi 7 — Māmiyaṃ Calitā Vilokya |
Summary
Now Kṛṣṇa Himself laments: "Seeing me surrounded by other women, Rādhā has gone; feeling guilty, I did not stop her. Alas, she has left hurt and angry." Without her, what use are wealth, people or home? He pictures her face with knitted brows. "She is in my heart; why do I search the forest?" Finally He pleads: "Forgive me; I will never do this again; grant me the sight of you."

Upanyasam take-away
The Lord of the universe says "kṣamyatām" — forgive me — to His devotee! This is the height of the Lord's love for His devotees (bhakta vātsalya). In true love there is no room for the ego that insists "I am right."
The fourth stanza holds another great truth: "tām ahaṃ hṛdi saṅgatām" — she is already in my heart, so why search outside? The Lord too is in our hearts; to experience this, the mind must turn back from its wandering in the world.

Detailed meaning — stanza by stanza

1. mAmiyam calitA vilOkya vrtam vadhU nicayEna…
Meaning: Seeing me surrounded by a crowd of women, she went away. Being at fault, and greatly afraid, I did not stop her. Alas, Hari! Feeling slighted, she has gone as if in anger.
Key message: Realising one's mistake begins remorse; realising how much it hurt the other is true purification.

2. kim kariSyati kim vadiSyati sA ciram virahENa…
Meaning: What will she do, what will she say, after this long separation? Without her, what use to me are wealth, people, ornaments or home?
Key message: Only when we lose what we most need do we see how small everything else is.

3. cintayAmi tadAnanam kuTilabhrU kOpa bharENa…
Meaning: I think of her face with brows knitted in anger — like a red lotus with a bee circling restlessly above it.
Key message: Even the anger of the beloved appears lovable.

4. tAmaham hrdi sangatAm anisham bhrsham ramayAmi…
Meaning: She is united with me in my heart, and I delight in her constantly. Then why do I search for her in the forest? Why do I lament in vain?
Key message: The Lord is not far away; He is in our own hearts.

5. tanvi khinnam asUyayA hrdayam tavAkalayAmi…
Meaning: Slender one, I understand how your heart is hurt by jealousy. But I do not know where you have gone, and so I cannot come to console you.
Key message: Understanding another's heart without being told is the greatness of love.

6. drshyasE puratO gatAgatamEva mE vidadhAsi…
Meaning: You seem to appear before me, coming and going; but why do you not embrace me eagerly as before?
Key message: In separation the beloved seems to appear and vanish; remembrance itself becomes a vision.

7. kSamyatAm aparam kadApi tavEdrsham na karOmi…
Meaning: Forgive me; I will never again do such a thing to you. Beautiful one, grant me the sight of you; I am tormented by Love.
Key message: The Lord of the universe asks His devotee's forgiveness — this is bhakta vātsalya.

8. varNitam jayadEvakEna harEridam pravaNEna…
Meaning: This state of Hari was described by the devoted Jayadeva, who arose like the moon from the ocean of Kindubilva.
Key message: Singing of the Lord's longing is itself a spiritual practice.

@@ 7.3 | Ślokam — Conclusion of the third sarga |
Summary
These six verses express Kṛṣṇa's pain of separation from Rādhā. He tells Love, "I am not Śiva; why do you attack me?" He says Rādhā's glances have already wounded his heart. He recalls her brows, glances, touch and sweet words. Finally comes a blessing: may the waves of glances that Madhusūdana, playing the flute, cast at Rādhā's moon-like face bring us well-being. Thus ends the third sarga, "Mugdha Madhusūdana".

Upanyasam take-away
Remembering the Lord does not stop at joy; it increases the longing to experience Him more closely. Kṛṣṇa Himself asks, "When my mind is absorbed in her, why does the pain of separation grow?" The more one remembers, the stronger the yearning for direct experience — this is the nature of loving devotion.

Detailed meaning — verse by verse

1. hrdi bisalatAhAra: nAyam bhujangama nAyaka:…
Meaning: O Love! On my chest is a garland of lotus stalks, not the king of serpents; on my neck a string of blue lotus petals, not the dark stain of poison; on my body sandal powder, not ash. Do not mistake me for Śiva and strike me, who am suffering without my beloved. Why do you rush at me in anger?
Key message: The cooling things the lovesick Kṛṣṇa wears make Him look like Śiva — such is the intensity of separation.

2. pANau mA kuru cUtasAyakamamum mA cApam ArOpaya…
Meaning: O Love, do not take up that mango-blossom arrow; do not string your bow. You who conquered the world in play, what heroism is there in striking one already fainting? My heart, shattered by the arrows of that doe-eyed one's glances, has not yet recovered at all.
Key message: A heart once struck by love has room for no other weapon.

3. bhrUpallavam dhanurapAnga tarangitAni…
Meaning: Her brow is the bow, her rippling side-glances the arrows, the edge of her ear the bowstring. Has Love handed over all his world-conquering weapons to her, the living goddess of his victory?
Key message: Rādhā is love embodied; all of Love's power rests in her.

4. bhrUcApE nihita: kaTAkSavishikhO nirmAtu marmavyathAm…
Meaning: Let the arrow of her glance, fixed on the bow of her brow, wound my heart; let her dark, curling braid aid Love's efforts; slender one, let your red lips (red, and full of passion) deepen my delusion — but why does your rounded (and virtuous) bosom play with my very life?
Key message: In separation, every aspect of the beloved comes to mind.

5. tAni sparsha sukhAni tE ca saraLA: snigdhA drshOvibhramA:…
Meaning: That joy of touch, those gentle movements of her eyes, the fragrance of her lotus face, the sweet curve of her nectar-dripping words, the sweetness of her lips — even when my mind is absorbed in her in meditation, alas, how does this sickness of separation keep growing?
Key message: Remembering the Lord brings joy and also deepens the longing to experience Him directly.

6. tiryakkaNTha vilOlamauli taraLOttamsasya vamshOccarat…
Meaning: With neck bent, crown swaying and earrings trembling, Madhusūdana played the flute; unnoticed by the countless women absorbed in its music, His waves of glances fell softly and lingered long on Rādhā's moon-like face. May those glances bring you well-being.
Key message: Even among thousands, the Lord's gaze rests on His intimate devotee.

@@ 8.1 | Ślokam — Opening of the fourth sarga |
Summary
The fourth sarga begins. Rādhā's companion approaches Mādhava, who sits dejected in a bower among the reeds on the Yamunā bank, bewildered by the weight of love, and speaks to Him.

Upanyasam take-away
The companion now becomes the messenger, building a bridge between the separated Rādhā and Mādhava. Tradition sees this companion as the ācārya, who conveys the soul's longing to the Lord and brings the two together. Every sincere thought of the Lord touches the Lord's own heart.

Detailed meaning

1. yamunAtIra vAnIra nikunjE mandamAsthitam…
Meaning: To Mādhava, sitting listlessly in a bower of reeds on the Yamunā's bank, bewildered by the weight of love, Rādhā's companion spoke.
Key message: The companion who conveys the devotee's state to the Lord stands in the place of the ācārya.

@@ 8.2 | Aṣṭapadi 8 — Nindati Candanam |
Summary
The companion describes Rādhā's suffering to Mādhava: "Mādhava, separated from you, Rādhā is wretched." Sandal and moonlight pain her; the Malaya breeze feels like poison. She uses wet lotus leaves as armour, prepares a bed of flowers for you like a vow, sheds tears, draws your image in musk and worships it, says repeatedly "I have fallen at your feet," and in meditation sees you before her, weeping and laughing.

Upanyasam take-away
"Tvayi līnā" — she is absorbed in you. When the mind is fully absorbed in the Lord, worldly pleasures — sandal, moon, breeze — lose all their taste.
The sixth stanza is the heart: "mādhava tava caraṇe patitāham" — I have fallen at your feet. Surrender is not only asking "solve my problem"; it is giving oneself up completely: "I cannot do it; I am yours." The seventh stanza shows that when the mind is absorbed in meditation, remembrance turns into experience.

Detailed meaning — stanza by stanza

1. nindati candanam indukiraNam anuvindati khEdam adhIram…
Meaning: Mādhava, she reviles sandal paste; she is unbearably pained by the moon's rays; she feels the Malaya breeze is poison, since it comes from the home of serpents. As if fearing Love's arrows, she is absorbed in you in contemplation. In separation from you she is wretched.
Key message: To a mind absorbed in the Lord, worldly pleasures lose their taste.

2. avirala nipatita madanasharAdiva bhavadavanAya vishAlam…
Meaning: As if to protect you (who dwell in her heart) from the ceaseless rain of Love's arrows, she makes a broad armour of wet lotus leaves over the tender core of her heart.
Key message: The devotee even tries to protect the Lord who dwells in the heart — such is the intimacy.

3. kusuma vishikha sharatalpam analpa vilAsa kalA kamanIyam…
Meaning: Like a vow, she prepares a bed of flowers — like Love's bed of arrows, lovely with many graces — for the joy of your embrace.
Key message: Every act done for the Lord turns from ordinary work into devotion.

4. vahati ca galita vilOcana jalabharam Anana kamalam udAram…
Meaning: Her lovely lotus face, streaming with tears, looks like the moon pouring streams of nectar when bitten by the fierce teeth of Rāhu.
Key message: True love sometimes goes beyond words and flows as tears.

5. vilikhati rahasi kurangamadEna bhavantam asamasharabhUtam…
Meaning: In secret she draws you in musk as the God of Love, with the makara emblem below and a fresh mango-blossom arrow in your hand, and bows to the image.
Key message: In separation the devotee gives the Lord a form and worships it — this is the principle of the worshipped image (arcā).

6. pratipadam idamapi nigadati mAdhava tava caraNE patitAham…
Meaning: At every moment she says: "Mādhava, I have fallen at your feet. When you turn away from me, even the nectar-rayed moon at once burns my body."
Key message: "I cannot do it; I am yours" — complete self-giving is surrender.

7. dhyAnalayEna pura: parikalpya bhavantam atIva durApam…
Meaning: Absorbed in meditation, imagining you — so hard to reach — before her, she laments, laughs, despairs, weeps, wanders, and for a moment is relieved of her pain.
Key message: When the mind rests in one divine thought, remembrance becomes direct experience.

8. shrI jayadEva bhaNitamidam adhikam yadi manasA naTanIyam…
Meaning: Those who wish to enact this in their minds should recite these words of the companion of the cowherd maiden distressed by separation from Hari, sung by Śrī Jayadeva.
Key message: The fruit of the Gīta Govindam is not only reading it but living its feeling in the heart.
@@ 9.1 | Ślokam |
Summary
The companion continues telling Mādhava: separated from you, Rādhā feels her home is a wilderness and her dear friends a trapping net; the heat of her body, fanned by her sighs, blazes like a forest fire. She has become like a frightened doe, and Love hunts her like a tiger.

Upanyasam take-away
When the mind holds on wholly to something, its absence makes the whole world unbearable. Rādhā has home, friends and every comfort — but not Kṛṣṇa. When the Lord becomes not just a part of life but its very meaning, separation from Him becomes the greatest pain.

Detailed meaning

1. AvAsO vipinAyatE priyasakhImAlA'pi jAlAyatE…
Meaning: Her dwelling seems a forest, her circle of dear friends a net; the heat of her body, fanned by her sighs, becomes like the flames of a forest fire. Alas, through separation from you she has become like a doe, and Love, like Yama, torments her as a tiger plays with its prey.
Key message: When the beloved is absent, all that should give joy gives sorrow.

@@ 9.2 | Aṣṭapadi 9 — Stana Vinihitamapi Hāram |
Summary
In each stanza the companion says, "Keśava, separated from you, Rādhikā is like this." The light necklace on her breast feels a heavy burden; she fears cool sandal paste as poison; her long sighs burn like Love's fire; she scatters teardrops in all directions; the bed of tender leaves seems like fire; she rests her cheek on her hand without moving; and she constantly repeats "Hari, Hari."

Upanyasam take-away
Jayadeva uses a striking device: everything meant to give comfort gives pain — the necklace is a burden, sandal is poison, breath is fire, the flower bed is a blaze. Comforts are not lacking; the mind to enjoy them is.
The seventh stanza is the climax: "hariritī haririti japati" — even in the last extremity of separation, the name of Hari is on Rādhā's lips. Though it comes through suffering, the chanting of the Lord's name leads finally to His feet. This is the glory of nāma saṅkīrtana.

Detailed meaning — stanza by stanza

1. stana vinihitamapi hAram udAram…
Meaning: Even the fine necklace placed on her breast feels to her, so thin and weak, like an unbearable burden. Keśava, in separation from you Rādhikā is like this.
Key message: When the mind is weary, even ornaments become a burden.

2. sarasa masrNamapi malayajapankam…
Meaning: Even moist, smooth sandal paste she regards with fear, as if it were poison on her body.
Key message: The sandal meant to cool becomes a torment in separation.

3. shvasitapavanam anupama pariNAham…
Meaning: She breathes out long, incomparable sighs that burn like the fire of Love.
Key message: In separation, even the breath that sustains life prolongs the pain.

4. dishidishi kirati sajalakaNa jAlam…
Meaning: From her eyes, like lotuses cut from their stalks, she scatters a network of teardrops in every direction.
Key message: Her eyes search for Kṛṣṇa; not finding Him, they overflow.

5. nayanaviSayamapi kisalaya talpam…
Meaning: Even the bed of tender leaves before her eyes she regards as a kindled fire.
Key message: Even the place of rest becomes fire for one in separation.

6. tyajati na pANitalEna kapOlam…
Meaning: She does not lift her hand from her cheek, pale and motionless like the evening moon.
Key message: In separation, body and mind become still, fixed on one thought.

7. haririti haririti japati sakAmam…
Meaning: Though near death from separation, she keeps repeating "Hari, Hari" with longing.
Key message: Even at the last, the name on her lips is Hari's — the glory of nāma saṅkīrtana.

8. shrI jayadEva bhaNitamiti gItam…
Meaning: May this song sung by Śrī Jayadeva bring happiness to those who have reached the feet of Keśava.
Key message: Though it sings of separation, its fruit is the joy of the Lord's feet.

@@ 9.3 | Ślokam — Conclusion of the fourth sarga |
Summary
The companion pleads with Kṛṣṇa: "She thrills, trembles and faints; only if you, like the physician of the gods, show grace will she live. Your touch is the only nectar that can heal her; if you withhold it, you are crueller than the thunderbolt." Sandal and moon do not cool her; she lives only by meditating on you. Finally comes a blessing: may the arm of Kṛṣṇa that lifted Govardhana bring us welfare. Thus ends the fourth sarga, "Snigdha Madhusūdana".

Upanyasam take-away
Knowing which medicine cures which illness is the first wisdom. Worldly objects cannot cure the fever of worldly existence; only the Lord's grace can — He is the "physician of the gods", the healer of the disease of saṃsāra.
When love for the Lord is real, it brings not only prayer but a sense of rightful claim — the companion boldly says, "If you do not help, you are cruel!"
The last verse is a lovely turn: after a whole sarga of tears and fever comes the arm that lifted Govardhana. The arm that protected Gokula is the arm that must protect Rādhā — and protects us too.

Detailed meaning — verse by verse

1. sA rOmAncati sItkarOti vilapati utkampatE tAmyati…
Meaning: She thrills, gasps, laments, trembles, grows faint, meditates, wanders in mind, closes her eyes, falls, rises, and even swoons. In so fierce a fever of love, will this fair one not live, O you who are like the physician of the gods, if you are pleased to show grace? Otherwise she is abandoned.
Key message: Knowing the right remedy is the first wisdom; the Lord is the remedy for the soul's suffering.

2. smarAturAm daivatavaidya hrdya…
Meaning: O you, dearer than the physicians of the gods! If you do not free from suffering Rādhā, tormented by Love and curable only by the nectar of your touch, then, Upendra, you are crueller than the thunderbolt.
Key message: Deep love brings not only prayer but a sense of rightful claim.

3. kandarpajvara sanjvarAkulatanO: Ashcaryam asyAshciram…
Meaning: Strangely, though long burning with Love's fever, her mind finds no relief in thinking of sandal, the moon or lotuses. But in her exhaustion she meditates in solitude on you alone, the cool-bodied beloved, and so, wasted as she is, somehow lives from moment to moment.
Key message: The wrong object cannot satisfy a true longing; meditation on the Lord sustains life.

4. kSaNamapi viraha: purA na sEhE…
Meaning: She who once could not bear even the moment's separation of your eyes blinking — how does she still breathe now, after such long separation, seeing the mango branch in full bloom?
Key message: The measure of love is not how much we remember the Lord but how much we long for Him in His absence.

5. vrSTi vyAkula gOkulAvanavashAt uddhrtya gOvardhanam…
Meaning: The arm of the cowherd lord, Kaṃsa's enemy, which lifted Govardhana to protect Gokula distressed by rain, which was long kissed by the delighted cowherd women, and which proudly bore the vermilion marks of their lips — may it bring you welfare.
Key message: The arm that protected Gokula is the arm that protects Rādhā — and us.

@@ 10.1 | Ślokam — Opening of the fifth sarga |
Summary
The fifth sarga begins. Hearing the companion, Kṛṣṇa, the slayer of Madhu, tells her: "I will stay here. Go to Rādhā, console her with my words and bring her here." Sent in this way, she goes to Rādhā and speaks to her again.

Upanyasam take-away
The Lord sends the companion as His messenger: "Bring her with my words." This is the ācārya's work — to carry the Lord's message and lead the soul to Him. "Aham iha nivasāmi" — "I will stay right here": the Lord has not gone anywhere; He waits for us to return to Him.

Detailed meaning

1. ahamiha nivasAmi yAhi rAdhAm…
Meaning: "I will stay here; go to Rādhā. Win her over with my words and bring her here." Commissioned thus by the slayer of Madhu, the companion herself went to Rādhā and spoke to her again.
Key message: The Lord waits; the ācārya brings His message and calls us to Him.

@@ 10.2 | Aṣṭapadi 10 — Vahati Malaya Samīre |
Summary
Now the companion tells Rādhā of Kṛṣṇa's suffering: "Friend Rādhā, separated from you, the wearer of the forest garland is wasting away." The Malaya breeze carries Love; flowers bloom only to break the hearts of those in separation. The cool moon burns Him; He covers His ears against the humming of bees; He suffers night after night; He has left His beautiful home to lie on the forest ground, repeatedly calling your name.

Upanyasam take-away
Throughout the fourth sarga the companion told Kṛṣṇa of Rādhā's longing. In this aṣṭapadi the same companion tells Rādhā of Kṛṣṇa's longing. As the devotee yearns for the Lord, the Lord yearns for the devotee — this is the heart of this aṣṭapadi.
The fourth stanza deserves attention: "bahu vilapati tava nāma" — the Lord Himself laments, calling the devotee's name! Greater than our chanting His name is His waiting while repeating ours. He leaves His splendid abode ("lalita dhāma") to wait in the forest for His devotee. All we need to do is go to Him.

Detailed meaning — stanza by stanza

1. vahati malaya samIrE madanam upanidhAya…
Meaning: The Malaya breeze blows, bearing Love with it; clusters of flowers burst open only to tear the hearts of those in separation. Friend Rādhā, at such a time, separated from you, the wearer of the forest garland is wasting away.
Key message: Not only the devotee — the Lord too yearns for the devotee.

2. dahati shishiramayUkhE maraNam anukarO'ti…
Meaning: When the cool-rayed moon burns Him, He seems to experience death itself; when Love's arrows fall, He laments in great distress.
Key message: When what should cool instead burns, that is separation — here it is the Lord's own.

3. dhvanati madhupasamUhE shravaNam apidadhAti…
Meaning: When the swarms of bees hum, He covers His ears; with His mind shaken by separation, He suffers night after night.
Key message: Without the beloved, even sweet sounds become unbearable.

4. vasati vipina vitAnE tyajati lalitadhAma…
Meaning: Leaving His lovely home, He dwells in a forest bower; rolling on the bare ground for a bed, He laments, repeating your name again and again.
Key message: The Lord Himself waits, repeating the devotee's name; all we must do is go to Him.

5. bhaNati kavi jayadEvE viraha vilasitEna…
Meaning: As the poet Jayadeva sings of this play of separation, may Hari arise, through merit, in the eager and blessed heart.
Key message: The merit of hearing of the Lord's longing makes Him rise in our hearts.

@@ 11.1 | Ślokam |
Summary
The companion tells Rādhā: "In the very bower where you and He once enjoyed love's bliss — a great pilgrimage place of Love — Mādhava now waits, meditating on you constantly, repeating your words like a string of mantras, longing again for the nectar of your embrace."

Upanyasam take-away
"Tavaiva ālāpa mantrāvalīṃ japan" — Kṛṣṇa repeats Rādhā's words as if they were mantras! As we chant the Lord's name, the Lord repeats the devotee's words. In true love, remembrance is not an effort; it becomes a natural japa.

Detailed meaning

1. pUrvam yatra samam tvayA ratipatE: ArAditA: siddhaya:…
Meaning: In that very bower — a great sacred place of Love — where He once attained with you the fulfilments of love, Mādhava now meditates on you without pause, repeating like a garland of mantras the words you spoke, and longs once more for the nectar of your close embrace.
Key message: The Lord too treasures the devotee's words like mantras — such is the intimacy.

@@ 11.2 | Aṣṭapadi 11 — Ratisukhasāre Gatam Abhisāre |
Summary
The companion urges Rādhā to go and meet her beloved: "In the forest on the Yamunā's bank, where the gentle breeze blows, the wearer of the forest garland waits for you; do not delay." He plays His flute signalling your name; He treasures even the dust raised by the breeze that touched you; whenever a bird alights or a leaf stirs, He thinks you are coming. She advises Rādhā to remove her jingling anklets and wear a dark garment for the night. "The night is ending; act on my words at once."

Upanyasam take-away
"Dhīra samīre yamunā tīre vasati vane vanamālī" is one of the most beloved refrains of the Gīta Govindam. The Lord waits; it is the soul that delays.
The third stanza is beautiful — at the stirring of a leaf the Lord looks down the path, thinking, "Is she coming?" When longing is deep, waiting itself becomes prayer.
The fourth stanza holds a secret of spiritual practice — "remove the jingling anklets." On the path to the Lord, the noise within is a greater obstacle than the noise of the world; one must go with a quiet mind.
The seventh stanza — "the night is ending." The night of life too is passing; good intentions must be acted upon at once.

Detailed meaning — stanza by stanza

1. ratisukhasArE gatam abhisArE madana manOhara vESam…
Meaning: He has gone to the meeting place, the essence of love's joy, dressed so charmingly that He enchants even Love. Lovely one, do not delay; follow the lord of your heart. In the forest on the Yamunā's bank, where the gentle breeze blows, the wearer of the forest garland waits.
Key message: Do not delay in going where your heart is already calling.

2. nAmasamEtam krtasankEtam vAdayatE mrduvENum…
Meaning: As a signal, He softly plays His flute, weaving in your name. He greatly prizes even the dust stirred by the breeze that has touched your body.
Key message: A thing's value lies in its connection — even dust that touched the devotee is precious to the Lord.

3. patati patatrE vicalati patrE shankita bhavadupayAnam…
Meaning: When a bird alights or a leaf stirs, He imagines you are coming. He prepares the bed and, with anxious eyes, watches the path you will take.
Key message: When longing is deep, waiting itself becomes prayer.

4. mukharam adhIram tyaja manjIram ripumiva kEliSu lOlam…
Meaning: Friend, take off these noisy, restless anklets, which betray you like an enemy in love's play. Go to the dark bower; put on a dark-blue cloak.
Key message: On the way to the Lord, the noise within is the greatest obstacle; go with a quiet mind.

5. urasi murArE: upahitahArE ghana iva tarala balAkE…
Meaning: On Murāri's chest, adorned with a necklace like a dark cloud with a row of flying cranes, you, golden-hued, will shine like lightning — the fruit of your merit.
Key message: Union with the Lord comes as the fruit of past merit.

6. vigalita vasanam parihrta rashanam ghaTaya jaghanam apidhAnam…
Meaning: Lotus-eyed one, go without hesitation, so that on the bed of tender leaves He may find you, like a treasure that is the source of joy.
Key message: The soul's complete self-offering to the Lord is the treasure of joy.

7. harirabhimAnI rajaniridAnIm iyam upayAti virAmam…
Meaning: Hari is proud and sensitive, and the night is now drawing to its end. Carry out my words quickly; fulfil the desire of the slayer of Madhu.
Key message: The night of life is passing; act on good intentions at once.

8. shrI jayadEvE krtaharisEvE bhaNati parama ramaNIyam…
Meaning: Śrī Jayadeva, who serves Hari, sings this most delightful song. Bow to Hari — joyful at heart, exceedingly compassionate, and attainable through merit.
Key message: A heart that approaches through love finally reaches Hari, the ocean of compassion.

@@ 11.3 | Ślokam — Conclusion of the fifth sarga |
Summary
The fifth sarga, "Sākāṅkṣa Puṇḍarīkākṣa", ends with five verses. The companion says: your beloved sighs again and again, watches the path, and prepares the bed. The sun has set and darkness has deepened; this is the right time to go. When separated lovers meet again, a joy mixed with shyness arises. May He feel fulfilled seeing you arrive, stepping fearfully through the dark. Finally comes a blessing: may Devakī's son, like a bee to Rādhā's lotus face, protect you.

Upanyasam take-away
We think we are seeking the Lord; in truth the Lord is waiting eagerly for us. The name of the sarga says it — "Sākāṅkṣa Puṇḍarīkākṣa": the lotus-eyed Lord full of longing.
The fourth verse is a beautiful teaching: in the dark and afraid, Rādhā stops by every tree and takes one step at a time. Even with fear, taking a step in the direction love calls is true courage. When we take one step, He feels fulfilled on seeing us.

Detailed meaning — verse by verse

1. vikirati muhu: shvAsAn AshA: purO muhurIkSatE…
Meaning: Dear one, your beloved sighs again and again; looks in every direction again and again; enters the bower again and again; murmurs something and grows very weary; prepares the bed again and again and looks out again anxiously. Exhausted by the torment of Love, this is how He is.
Key message: A mind that truly waits for someone cannot be still.

2. tvadvAkyE na samam samagram adhunA tigmAmshu: astamgata:…
Meaning: Together with your contrariness the sun has now set completely; together with Govinda's longing, the darkness has deepened. My pleading has grown long, like the mournful cry of the cakravāka birds. Lovely one, further delay is useless; this is the delightful moment for the meeting.
Key message: Recognising the right moment and acting on it is also wisdom.

3. AshlESAt anucumbanAt anunakhOllEkhAt anusvAntaja-…
Meaning: Embrace, then kisses, then nail-marks, then the stirring of love within, then excitement, then union — for lovers delighted thus, who had gone out on other errands, met by chance in the dark and recognised each other by their voices, what joy mingled with shyness does not arise in the darkness!
Key message: Separation is caused not by lack of love but by misunderstanding; honest, open conversation removes it.

4. sabhaya cakitam vinyasyantIm drsham timirE pathi…
Meaning: Lovely-faced one, casting fearful, startled glances on the dark path, stopping by every tree and slowly placing your steps, you will somehow reach the secret place with limbs rippling with love. Seeing you, may that handsome one feel fulfilled.
Key message: Even with fear, a step in the direction love calls is true courage.

5. rAdhA mugdha mukhAravinda madhupa: trailOkya maulisthalI…
Meaning: The bee to Rādhā's lovely lotus face; the crest-jewel of the three worlds; a blue gem fit for adornment; descended to lighten the earth's burden; like the evening that delights the hearts of Vraja's beauties; the comet of Kaṃsa's destruction — may that son of Devakī protect you.
Key message: The Lord is not far away; He waits in the direction our heart must turn.

@@ 12.1 | Ślokam — Opening of the sixth sarga |
Summary
The sixth sarga begins. Seeing that Rādhā, long devoted to Kṛṣṇa, is too weak to go to Him and lies in the bower of creepers, the companion goes to Govinda, who is weary with love, and tells Him of her condition.

Upanyasam take-away
Rādhā wants to go, but separation has weakened her. Then the companion — the ācārya — conveys this very condition to the Lord. When we cannot go to the Lord ourselves, the ācārya intercedes for us. True love carries the mind even where the body cannot go.

Detailed meaning

1. atha tAm gantum ashaktAm ciram anuraktAm latAgrhE drSTvA…
Meaning: Then, seeing her in the bower of creepers — long devoted, yet unable to go — the companion told her state to Govinda, who was languishing with love.
Key message: When we are unable, the ācārya presents our condition to the Lord.

@@ 12.2 | Aṣṭapadi 12 — Paśyati Diśidiśi |
Summary
The companion tells Kṛṣṇa: "Lord! Hari! Lord of the world! Rādhā languishes in her dwelling." In solitude she sees you in every direction; she sets out eagerly to meet you but falls after a few steps; wearing lotus stalks as bangles, she lives only by the memory of your love; she imagines "I am Madhuripu"; she asks her friend repeatedly why Hari does not come; she embraces the darkness, thinking it is you; and as you delay, she weeps without shame.

Upanyasam take-away
The fourth stanza is profound: "madhuripur aham iti bhāvanaśīlā" — she imagines that she herself is Madhuripu. Through constant meditation, the meditator and the object of meditation become one — the state of tanmayatā. The gopīs, thinking only of Kṛṣṇa, even enacted His deeds; this is that state.
The sixth stanza — she embraces even the darkness, thinking "Hari has come." When the mind is filled with the Lord, every sight in the world reveals Him. "Nātha hare jagannātha hare" — the Lord of the whole world listens to the pain of one devotee.

Detailed meaning — stanza by stanza

1. pashyati dishidishi rahasi bhavantam…
Meaning: In solitude she sees you in every direction, drinking the honey of (others') sweet lips. Lord! Hari! Lord of the world! Rādhā languishes in her dwelling.
Key message: Though unseen by the eye, He appears everywhere to a mind full of love.

2. tvadabhisaraNa rabhasEna valantI…
Meaning: Setting out in her eagerness to meet you, she falls after only a few steps.
Key message: Love urges her to go; separation holds the body back.

3. vihita vishada bisa kisalaya valayA…
Meaning: Wearing bangles of white lotus stalks and tender shoots, she survives here only by the memory of your love.
Key message: The memory of experiencing the Lord can itself sustain life.

4. muhuravalOkita maNDana lIlA…
Meaning: Looking again and again at her ornaments, she imagines, "I am Madhuripu."
Key message: Through unbroken meditation the meditator and the meditated become one — tanmayatā.

5. tvaritam upaiti na katham abhisAram…
Meaning: She asks her friend again and again, "Why does Hari not come quickly to the meeting place?"
Key message: When love is deep, the distance before meeting becomes unbearable.

6. shliSyati cumbati jaladhara kalpam…
Meaning: Seeing the thick darkness like a rain cloud, she thinks "Hari has come," and embraces and kisses it.
Key message: When the mind is full of the Lord, we see Him in everything.

7. bhavati vilambini vigalita lajjA…
Meaning: As you delay, she who has adorned herself and waits for you abandons shyness, laments and weeps.
Key message: The deeper the expectation, the deeper the pain of delay.

8. shrI jayadEva kavEridam uditam…
Meaning: May this song of the poet Śrī Jayadeva bring great joy to those who can relish it.
Key message: A mind that tastes divine love becomes full of joy.

@@ 12.3 | Ślokam — Conclusion of the sixth sarga |
Summary
The sixth sarga, "Kuṇṭha Vaikuṇṭha", ends with three verses. The companion says: "Deceiver! Thinking of you, Rādhā thrills, falters in speech, and lies absorbed in meditation in an ocean of love." Whenever a leaf stirs she adorns herself and prepares the bed thinking you have come; without you she will not survive this night. The last verse is a benediction on the glory of words concerning Govinda.

Upanyasam take-away
Remembrance, when it deepens, becomes meditation; meditation, when it deepens, becomes experience. Though one may do a hundred things outwardly — adorning, preparing the bed, making plans — what we hold within is the true direction of our life.
Trying to remember the Lord is the beginning of devotion; being unable to stop remembering Him is its depth. Even on a day when the body cannot reach Him, if the mind is with Him, that day too is a day of devotion.

Detailed meaning — verse by verse

1. vipula pulaka pAli sphIta sItkAram anta:…
Meaning: Deceiver! Thinking intensely of you with love, the doe-eyed one thrills all over, sighs deeply, speaks in a voice faltering with the numbness within, and, immersed in an ocean of feeling, remains absorbed in meditation.
Key message: Remembrance deepens into meditation, and meditation into experience.

2. angESu AbharaNam karOti bahusha: patrE'pi sancAriNi…
Meaning: Whenever even a leaf stirs, suspecting that you have come, she adorns her limbs again and again, spreads the bed, and meditates for a long time. Though engaged in a hundred such acts of adorning, doubting, preparing the bed and imagining, the lovely one will not pass this night without you.
Key message: Whatever we do outwardly, what we hold within is the true direction of life.

3. kim vishrAmyasi krSNa bhOgibhavanE bhANDIra bhUmIruhi…
Meaning: "Kṛṣṇa, why do you rest beneath the bhāṇḍīra tree near the serpent's pool? Brother, go home to Nanda's joyful house, which is out of sight from here" — Rādhā's words, carried by a traveller and repeated by a cowherd near Nanda in the evening, praising the hospitality of the guest Govinda: may those words be victorious.
Key message: Commentators give many subtle readings of this verse; its core is that Rādhā's message reaches Kṛṣṇa through a messenger. A devotee's plea will reach the Lord one way or another.
@@ 13.1 | Ślokam — Opening of the seventh sarga |
Summary
The seventh sarga begins. Meanwhile the moon rises like a sandal mark on the face of the beautiful directions and lights up Vṛndāvana. As the moon climbs higher and Mādhava still delays, Rādhā, distressed by separation, laments in many ways and cries out in grief.

Upanyasam take-away
Everything is ready for the meeting — moonlight, night, forest — except Kṛṣṇa. When the awaited one is absent, even a beautiful world feels empty. Jayadeva, in Rādhā's mood, sees even the moon's dark spot as a "mark of sin" — where love is great, even a small delay turns into a great doubt.

Detailed meaning — verse by verse

1. atrAntarE ca kulaTAkula vartmaghAta…
Meaning: Meanwhile the moon — bearing a clear dark mark, as if from the sin of blocking the path of wayward women, and like a sandal dot on the faces of the beautiful directions — lit up the interior of Vṛndāvana with its rays.
Key message: We give meaning to what we see according to our state of mind.

2. prasarati shashadharabimbE vihitavilambE ca mAdhavE vidurA…
Meaning: As the moon's orb spread its light and Mādhava delayed, Rādhā, stricken by separation, lamented in many ways and voiced her grief aloud.
Key message: When the awaited one is absent, even a beautiful world feels empty.

@@ 13.2 | Aṣṭapadi 13 — Kathita Samaye'pi Hariḥ |
Summary
Rādhā laments: "Even at the appointed time Hari has not come to the forest! My beauty and youth are wasted. Deceived by my friends' words, to whom shall I turn for refuge?" She came into the dark forest for Him; now death seems better. Ornaments and garlands torment her; the sweet spring night pains her; she suspects that some more fortunate woman is enjoying Hari. "I wait here; does Madhusūdana even think of me?"

Upanyasam take-away
"Yāmi he kam iha śaraṇam" — to whom shall I go for refuge? This is the question the soul asks when every support has failed. Jayadeva gives the answer himself in the last stanza: "haricaraṇa śaraṇa" — the feet of Hari are the refuge.
The seventh stanza is deep: "smarati madhusūdano mām api na cetasā" — does He even remember me? The greatest need in love is the assurance that we too dwell in the heart of the one we love. With the Lord this assurance is always present — in the earlier aṣṭapadis we saw Him repeating Rādhā's name as He waited.

Detailed meaning — stanza by stanza

1. kathita samayE'pi hari: ahaha na yayau vanam…
Meaning: Even at the promised time, alas, Hari did not come to the forest. My youth, with its spotless beauty, is wasted. Deceived by my friends' words, to whom shall I turn for refuge here?
Key message: Pain comes not only from events but from the expectations we build around them.

2. yadanugamanAya nishi gahanamapi shIlitam…
Meaning: The one for whom I entered even the dense forest at night has pierced my heart with Love's arrows.
Key message: Crossing outer obstacles is easy; crossing the inner pain of broken hope is hard.

3. mama maraNamEva varam ativitatha kEtanA…
Meaning: After such a vain effort, death is better for me. Senseless as I am, how can I bear this fire of separation?
Key message: In that moment pain seems to be the whole world, but it too will pass.

4. ahaha kalayAmi valayAdi maNibhUSaNam…
Meaning: Alas! Bearing the fire of separation from Hari, I find my bangles and jewelled ornaments a great torment.
Key message: When there is no joy within, outer beauty gives no delight.

5. mAm iha hi vidhurayati madhura madhuyAminI…
Meaning: This sweet spring night torments me here, while some woman who has earned merit enjoys Hari.
Key message: The thought that another receives the love we long for is a great pain.

6. kusuma sukumAratanum asama sharalIlayA…
Meaning: Through Love's play, even the flower garland on my breast cruelly strikes my body, soft as a flower.
Key message: When the inner state changes, the same thing can give joy or pain.

@@ 16.1 | Ślokam |

@@ 16.2 | Aṣṭapadi 16 — Anilatarala Kuvalaya Nayanena |
Summary

@@ 16.3 | Ślokam — Conclusion of the seventh sarga |
Summary

@@ 17.1 | Ślokam — Opening of the eighth sarga |

@@ 17.2 | Aṣṭapadi 17 — Rajani Janita Guru Jāgara |
Summary
This is the angry lament of the "khaṇḍitā" heroine — the one who has been deceived and hurt. Rādhā tells Kṛṣṇa: "Go, Mādhava! Go, Keśava! Do not speak deceitful words; go to her who relieves your sorrow." She points out His eyes red from a sleepless night, the dark kohl on His lips, nail-marks on His body, another woman's red lac on His chest, and tooth-marks on His lip. "If your outside is so stained, your heart must be too. What wonder that you roam the forests seeking women? Your nature was shown when you killed Pūtanā!"

Upanyasam take-away
In the tradition, Rādhā's anger (māna) is honoured as a high stage of love. Where there is no sense of belonging there is no anger; being able to be angry with the Lord so freely is a sign of intimacy.
Another point: once trust is broken, even a true explanation sounds like a lie. Rādhā is not ready to listen — "mā vada kaitava vādam", do not speak falsehoods. The Lord does not argue; He stands patiently. And Jayadeva calls this lament a nectar rare even for the gods — even the devotee's anger is sweet to the Lord.

Detailed meaning — stanza by stanza

1. rajani janita guru jAgara rAga kaSAyitam alasa nimESam…
Meaning: Your eyes, reddened by the long night's waking and blinking languidly, seem to carry the passion that has come to the surface. Hari, Hari! Go, Mādhava! Go, Keśava! Do not speak deceitful words. Lotus-eyed one, follow her who removes your sorrow.
Key message: Anger arises only where there is a sense of belonging; this too is a mood of love.

2. kajjala malina vilOcana cumbana viracita nIlimarUpam…
Meaning: Kṛṣṇa, your red lip, darkened by kissing eyes lined with kohl, has become fitting for your (dark) body.
Key message: Even in anger, Rādhā's words carry wit and love.

3. vapuranusarati tava smarasangara khara nakhara kSatarEkham…
Meaning: Your body, bearing the sharp nail-marks of love's battle, looks like a victory inscription written in gold on a slab of emerald.
Key message: When the heart is hurt, every sign looks like an accusation.

4. caraNakamala galat alaktaka siktam idam tava hrdayam udAram…
Meaning: Your broad chest, wet with red lac dripping from (another's) lotus feet, seems to show outwardly the fresh shoots of the tree of love growing within.
Key message: (A traditional reading) The Lord holds even the devotee's feet to His chest.

5. dashana padam bhavat adhara gatam mama janayati cEtasi khEdam…

The first verse is the peak of anger; the second turns suddenly to joy. Jayadeva closes every sarga on an auspicious note. @@ 18.2 | Aṣṭapadi 18 — Hariḥ Abhisarati |

In this hour of the sweet spring breeze, Hari Himself comes to meet you — what greater joy is there in the world? Why waste your beauty? The fifth stanza is beautiful — "saphalaya nayane": make your eyes fruitful by seeing Hari. The purpose of the eyes is the vision of the Lord. The sixth stanza — we create our own sorrow. Forgiving is not only for the other; it frees our own heart.

@@ 18.3 | Ślokam — Conclusion of the ninth sarga |
Summary
The ninth sarga, "Manda Mukunda", ends with two verses. In the first the companion scolds: "Harsh to the loving, stubborn to the one who bows, hostile to the passionate, turning away from the one who comes to you — for one who acts so perversely, it is fitting that sandal becomes poison, the moon becomes the sun, snow becomes fire!" The second is a benediction: may the flute-song of Kaṃsa's enemy bring you welfare.

Upanyasam take-away
"Viparītakāriṇi" — you who act in reverse! When the Lord comes lovingly and we turn away, even the sweet things of the world turn bitter. Joy and sorrow depend not on external objects but on our attitude to the Lord.
The last verse brings the flute: the great mantra that draws the gopīs and relieves the gods' distress. Even amid the quarrel, Jayadeva leads us to the flute-song — that sound is what draws the soul to the Lord.

Detailed meaning — verse by verse

1. snigdhE yat paruSAsi yatpraNamati stabdhAsi yadrAgiNi…
Meaning: You are harsh to one who is loving, stubborn when He bows, hostile to one full of love, and you turn away from your beloved when He comes towards you. For one acting so contrarily, it is only fitting that sandal paste becomes poison, the cool moon the sun, snow fire, and the joys of play torments.
Key message: When we turn away from the Lord, even sweet things become bitter.

2. antarmOhana mauli ghUrNanamilan mandAra visramsana…
Meaning: The flute-song of Kaṃsa's enemy — which enchants within; which makes mandāra flowers fall as His head sways; the great mantra that makes the eyes of the doe-eyed women fixed and festive; which destroys the unbearable distress of the gods troubled by arrogant demons — may it bring you welfare.
Key message: The flute-song is the great mantra that draws the soul to the Lord.

@@ 19.1 | Ślokam — Opening of the tenth sarga |
Summary
The tenth sarga begins. At dusk Hari approaches the lovely Rādhā — gently angry, her face weary from endless sighing, glancing shyly at her friend's face — and speaks in a voice choked with joy.

Upanyasam take-away
Now the Lord Himself comes down to the devotee. The sarga's name is "Catura Caturbhuja" — the clever four-armed Lord. He wins not by argument but by humbling Himself and touching the devotee's heart with loving words. One who wins in love is not the one who wins the argument, but the one who sets aside ego and touches the other's heart.

Detailed meaning

1. atrAntarE masrNa rOSavashAt asIma…
Meaning: Meanwhile, at the close of day, approaching the lovely Rādhā — her face weary from endless sighs under the sway of gentle anger, glancing shyly at her friend's face — Hari spoke these words in a voice choked with joy.
Key message: The Lord humbles Himself and comes down to the devotee.

@@ 19.2 | Aṣṭapadi 19 — Vadasi Yadi Kiñcidapi |
Summary
Kṛṣṇa pleads with Rādhā: "Dear one, gracious one, give up this groundless anger towards me." If you speak even a little, your smile will dispel my darkness; if you are truly angry, punish me, but do not stay apart; you are my life, my ornament, the jewel I found in the ocean of worldly existence. Finally — "dehi padapallavam udāram" — place your tender foot on my head as an ornament; let it remove the poison of Love.

Upanyasam take-away
The third stanza — "tvam asi mama jīvanaṃ tvam asi mama bhūṣaṇaṃ tvam asi mama bhavajaladhiratnam" — these are the Lord's words to the devotee! What the devotee should say to the Lord, the Lord says to the devotee.
The seventh stanza is the summit of the Gīta Govindam — "smara garala khaṇḍanaṃ mama śirasi maṇḍanaṃ dehi padapallavam udāram". A famous tradition tells that Jayadeva hesitated — "Rādhā's foot on Kṛṣṇa's head? How can I write this?" — stopped writing and went to bathe. In his absence Kṛṣṇa Himself came in Jayadeva's form, wrote the line, ate the food Padmāvatī served and left. Returning, Jayadeva found the line written on the palm leaf and wept. The devotee thinks he is writing; in truth the Lord holds his hand and writes.
The devotee's foot even on the Lord's head — this is the limit of the Lord's dependence on His devotees. "Ahaṃ bhakta-parādhīnaḥ" — "I am subject to My devotees," as He Himself says in the Bhāgavatam.

Detailed meaning — stanza by stanza

1. vadasi yadi kincidapi dantaruci kaumudI…
Meaning: If you say even a little, the moonlight of your teeth will dispel the dreadful darkness of my mind. The moon of your face, eager for the nectar of your shining lips, entices the cakora birds of my eyes. Dear one, gracious one, give up your groundless anger towards me. The fire of Love burns my heart; give me the honey of your lotus face to drink.
Key message: A loving smile can do what long explanations cannot.

2. satyamEvAsi yadi sudati mayi kOpinI…
Meaning: Fair-toothed one, if you are truly angry with me, strike me with your sharp nails, bind me with your arms, bite me with your teeth — do whatever gives you joy.
Key message: Expressing and dissolving anger preserves a relationship better than suppressing it and staying apart.

3. tvamasi mama jIvanam tvamasi mama bhUSaNam…
Meaning: You are my life; you are my ornament; you are the jewel I found in the ocean of existence. May you always be kindly towards me — for this my heart strives with all its effort.
Key message: What the devotee should say to the Lord, the Lord says to the devotee.

4. nIlanalinAbhamapi tanvi tava lOcanam…
Meaning: Slender one, your eyes, blue as blue lotuses, have taken on the hue of red lotuses (in anger). If they colour me as arrows of Love, that is only fitting for Kṛṣṇa.
Key message: Even the beloved's angry glance is lovable.

5. sphuratu kucakumbhayO: upari maNimanjarI…
Meaning: Let the jewelled necklace sparkle on your breast and adorn the region of your heart; let your girdle ring and proclaim the command of Love.
Key message: Let the love within show in outer adornment too.

6. sthala kamala bhanjanam mama hrdaya ranjanam…
Meaning: Sweet-spoken one, tell me — shall I adorn with red lac your two feet, which surpass the land-lotus, delight my heart and lend colour to love's play?
Key message: In true love, the great one does not hesitate to bow low.

7. smaragarala khaNDanam mama shirasi maNDanam…
Meaning: Place on my head, as its ornament, your generous tender foot, which destroys the poison of Love. Let it remove the burning of the cruel fire of Love blazing within me.
Key message: The devotee's foot on the Lord's head — the height of the Lord's dependence on His devotees.

8. iti caTula cATupaTu cAru muravairiNO…
Meaning: Thus the clever, charming, persuasive words spoken by Murāri to Rādhā are victorious. May this song too, the speech of the poet Jayadeva, beloved of Padmāvatī, be victorious.
Key message: Loving words gently open even a hardened heart.

@@ 19.3 | Ślokam — Conclusion of the tenth sarga |
Summary
The tenth sarga, "Catura Caturbhuja", ends with seven verses. Kṛṣṇa consoles Rādhā: "In my heart there is room for no one but you; give up your doubt." "Show your anger on me; punish me with your arms." "Your brow is like a serpent; only the nectar of your lips is its antidote." "Your silence pains me; speak." He praises her beauty, comparing it to flowers and to celestial maidens. Finally comes a benediction: may Hari, who fought the elephant Kuvalayāpīḍa, grant you joy.

Upanyasam take-away
The first verse is the essence — "parānavakāśini": in my heart there is no room for another. Security in a relationship comes not only from "there is no one else" but from the assurance "you matter most to me."
The fifth verse — "vyathayati vṛthā maunam": silence is the greatest punishment. In a quarrel, withdrawing into silence hurts more than anger.
To succeed in a relationship you need not prove who is right; it is enough to realise whom you must not lose. Winning in love is not defeating the other; it is defeating the ego that stands between the two.

Detailed meaning — verse by verse

1. parihara krtAtankE shankAm tvayA satatam ghana-…
Meaning: Fearful one, give up your doubt. In my heart, always filled with you and leaving no room for another, no fortunate one other than Love has entered. Grant me permission to begin your embrace.
Key message: The assurance "you are my whole heart" is the security of love.

2. mugdhE vidhEhi mayi nirdaya dantadamsha…
Meaning: Lovely one, bite me without mercy, bind me with your creeper-like arms, press me close. Fierce one, take your pleasure, so that my life is not lost to the cruel arrows of Love.
Key message: Even the pain within love is sweeter than the pain of separation.

3. shashimukhi tava bhAti bhangurabhrU:…
Meaning: Moon-faced one, your curved brow shines like a terrible black serpent that bewitches the young. To remove the fear it causes, the nectar of your lips alone is the perfect mantra.
Key message: The one we love most can both wound and heal us.

4. bandhUkadyuti bAndhavO'yam adhara: snigdhO madhUkacchavi:…
Meaning: Your lip is kin to the bandhūka flower's glow; your cheek has the hue of the madhūka flower; your eye steals the beauty of the blue lotus; your nose resembles the sesame flower; your teeth are like kunda blossoms. Dear one, the flower-arrowed god of Love conquers the world surely by serving your face.
Key message: Words of praise that open the heart are stronger than words of blame.

5. vyathayati vrthA maunam tanvi prapancaya pancamam…
Meaning: Slender one, your needless silence pains me; speak sweetly in the fifth note. Young one, relieve my pain with sweet words and glances. Lovely one, give up turning away; do not abandon me. Here I am, your most loving beloved.

6. drshau tava madAlasE vadanam indum atyunnatam…
Meaning: Your eyes are languid with passion (Madālasā); your face is a lofty moon (Indumatī); your gait delights the heart (Manoramā); your thighs surpass the plantain (Rambhā); your love is artful (Kalāvatī); your brows are a lovely picture (Citralekhā). Ah, slender one, though on earth, you carry the beauty of all the celestial maidens.
Key message: Helping the beloved feel their worth is also a form of love.

7. prItim vastanutAm hari: kuvalayApIDEna sArdham raNE…
Meaning: In the battle with the elephant Kuvalayāpīḍa, seeing its frontal globes, Hari recalled Rādhā's breasts, perspired and closed His eyes for a moment; Kaṃsa's men cried out "We have won, we have won!" — but the very next moment He felled the elephant. May that Hari grant you joy.
Key message: Even on the battlefield the Lord's mind is on His devotee — and in the same instant He defeats adharma.

@@ 20.1 | Ślokam — Opening of the eleventh sarga |
Summary

The quarrel is over; not a trace of sorrow remains on Rādhā's face. The sarga's name is "Sānanda Dāmodara" — the joyful Dāmodara. Success in a relationship is not one defeating the other; it is both finding each other again. The hour of union between the soul and the Supreme draws near.

@@ 20.2 | Aṣṭapadi 20 — Viracita Cāṭu Vacanaracanam |
Summary
The companion encourages Rādhā: "Lovely Rādhā, Madhumathana, who spoke sweet words and bowed at your feet, has now gone to the bed in the beautiful vañjula bower; follow Him." Walk slowly like a swan with your jewelled anklets ringing; listen to the sweet call of the cuckoos; even the creepers swaying in the breeze beckon you; your thrill announces His embrace; all your friends know — give up shyness and go; hold your friend's hand and let your bangles announce your coming to Him.

Earlier (in Aṣṭapadi 11), when the companion called, Rādhā did not go; pride and hesitation held her back. Now that her mind has changed, the same path becomes a beautiful journey — all of nature, cuckoo, creeper and breeze, sends her to Him. Once the mind turns towards the Lord, the whole world assists the journey.
"Anugatam anusara" — follow Him who followed you. The Lord came seeking us; now we must follow Him. The greatest enemy of love is not outside — it is our hesitation and ego.

@@ 20.3 | Ślokam |
Summary
Three verses. In the first the companion says: "Thinking 'she will see me, speak to me, embrace me, delight me,' your beloved waits anxiously in the dark bower; seeing you (in his mind) he trembles, thrills, rejoices, goes forward to meet you, and swoons." In the second, the darkness itself becomes the ornaments of the women going to meet their lovers — kohl, ear-flowers, hair-garland, musk. The first verse is very beautiful: the Lord longs for us even more than we long for Him; just thinking of our coming, He thrills.
The third verse holds a deep teaching: darkness — trials — does not come to destroy love; like a touchstone testing gold, it reveals true love. One sincere step we take towards the Lord quickens the steps He takes towards us.

@@ 21.2 | Aṣṭapadi 21 — Mañjutara Kuñjatala Kelisadane |

The companion sends Rādhā in: "Rādhā, enter into Mādhava's presence; bring Murāri a hundred blessings." Thinking of the Lord from a distance is one stage; at some point one must go to Him and surrender — that is prapatti.
Another point: the whole bower — leaves, breeze, flowers, cuckoos, bees — is ready to welcome Rādhā. In the Lord's presence everything waits to welcome the soul.

@@ 22.2 | Aṣṭapadi 22 — Rādhā Vadana Vilokana |
Summary
This describes the divine beauty of Hari as Rādhā sees Him on entering: "She saw Hari, single-minded in His long-desired play, His face overcome with joy and showing the stirrings of love." Seeing Rādhā's face, His heart surged like the ocean at the sight of the moon; the long pearl necklace on His chest was like foam on the Yamunā; His golden silk on His dark body like a blue lotus ringed with yellow pollen; His glancing eyes like wagtails playing in an autumn pond; His earrings like the sun; His sandal mark; His jewelled ornaments — Jayadeva describes each beauty.

Words cannot hold the joy of reaching what one has truly longed for; so Jayadeva leaves plain words and paints that beauty in images — ocean, Yamunā, blue lotus, wagtails, sun, moon. @@ 22.3 | Ślokam — Conclusion of the eleventh sarga |
Summary
The eleventh sarga, "Sānanda Govinda", ends with three verses. In the first, as Rādhā sees her beloved, tears of joy flow from her eyes like perspiration, from the effort of stretching to her ears to see Him. In the second, the friends leave, and when Rādhā sees Kṛṣṇa's face, her very shyness, out of shame, runs far away. The third is a benediction: may Murāri's arm be victorious, which, after felling the elephant Kuvalayāpīḍa, looked as if adorned with mandāra flowers by the drops of blood.

Upanyasam take-away
First verse: true joy cannot be hidden; when the heart is full, tears of joy bear witness (ānanda bāṣpa). Second: when true love comes, pretence and shyness vanish; the soul returns to its natural state. Third: the arm that embraces Rādhā is the same arm that defeats adharma — gentleness that embraces and strength that protects, in one Lord.
Rādhā went close to Kṛṣṇa; in truth Kṛṣṇa entered Rādhā's heart — this is the supreme secret of devotion.

Detailed meaning — verse by verse

1. atikramya apAngam shravaNapatha paryantagamana-…
Meaning: From her eyes, made restless by the very effort of reaching past their corners to the edge of her ears, a flood of joyful tears fell from Rādhā like perspiration at the moment of seeing her beloved.
Key message: When the heart is full, tears of joy bear witness.

2. bhajantyA: talpAntam krtakapaTa kaNDUtivihita-…
Meaning: As she approached the bed, the friends, hiding their smiles by pretending to scratch themselves, left the house. When the doe-eyed one saw her beloved's face, lovely with the signs of Love's arrows, even shyness, ashamed, went far away from her.
Key message: When true love comes, pretence vanishes and the soul returns to its natural state.

3. jayashrI vinyastai: mahita iva mandAra kusumai:…
Meaning: Victorious is the mighty arm of Murāri, which, with its scattered drops of blood from the sport of crushing the elephant Kuvalayāpīḍa, looked as if honoured with mandāra flowers placed by the goddess of victory, and as if marked with vermilion in the joy of the elephant fight.
Key message: The gentleness that embraces and the strength that protects are in the same Lord.

@@ 23.1 | Ślokam — Opening of the twelfth sarga |
Summary
The twelfth and final sarga begins. The friends have gone. Rādhā, her shyness lessening, her lips moist with a smile overflowing with love, languid with feeling, sits on the bed of fresh leaves with lowered eyes; Kṛṣṇa, gazing at her again and again with longing, speaks to her.

Upanyasam take-away
After long separation, doubt and quarrel — here at last is union. In true love anger does not last; a single moment of meeting dissolves all past pain. The subject of this sarga is the union of the soul with the Supreme — "Suprīta Pītāmbara".

Detailed meaning

1. gatavati sakhIvrndE mandatrapAbhara nirbhara-…
Meaning: When the friends had gone, Hari, seeing again and again His delighted beloved — her shyness lessened, her lips moist with a smile swelling with love, languid with feeling, eyes lowered on the bed of fresh leaves — spoke to her.
Key message: One moment of reunion dissolves all past pain.

@@ 23.2 | Aṣṭapadi 23 — Kisalaya Śayanatale |
Summary
Kṛṣṇa lovingly pleads with Rādhā: "Rādhā, at this very moment, enjoy Nārāyaṇa, who has followed you." Place your lotus feet on this bed of leaves; you have walked far — let me press your feet with my hands; speak words sweet as nectar; give me your rare embrace; revive this servant who is as good as dead; look at me, distraught by your needless anger; give up your anger.

Upanyasam take-away
"Kṣaṇam adhunā nārāyaṇam anugatam anubhaja rādhe" — here Jayadeva calls Kṛṣṇa "Nārāyaṇa"! This cowherd who followed Rādhā is none other than Śrīman Nārāyaṇa Himself. The hero of the Daśāvatāra in the first aṣṭapadi is the one who now presses His devotee's feet — "karakamalena karomi caraṇam".
In the fifth stanza the Lord calls Himself "dāsa", a servant. The height of love is not "you are mine" but "I am yours." True greatness in love lies not in ruling but in following.

Detailed meaning — stanza by stanza

1. kisalaya shayanatalE kuru kAmini caraNa naLina vinivEsham…
Meaning: Beloved, place your lotus feet on this bed of tender leaves; let the bed, rival of your leaf-like feet, now enjoy their beauty. Rādhā, at this very moment, enjoy Nārāyaṇa, who has followed you.
Key message: Greatness in love lies not in ruling but in following.

2. karakamalEna karOmi caraNamaham AgamitAsi vidUram…
Meaning: You have come a long way; let me press your feet with my lotus hand. Like the anklet that followed me, accept me too on this bed for a moment.
Key message: The Lord Himself bears the devotee's weariness — Nārāyaṇa pressing His devotee's feet.

3. vadana sudhAnidhi gaLitam amrtamiva racaya vacanam anukUlam…
Meaning: Speak kind words like nectar flowing from the moon of your face. As if to remove our separation, I remove the cloth that covers your breast.
Key message: The first step of reunion is removing the distance between hearts.

4. priya parirambhaNa rabhasa valitamiva puLakitam atiduravApam…
Meaning: Place on my chest your touch — hard to attain, thrilling as if with eagerness for your beloved's embrace — and calm the burning of my love.
Key message: Some pain cannot be cured by words; love's embrace is the cure.

5. adhara sudhArasam upanaya bhAmini jIvaya mrtamiva dAsam…
Meaning: Passionate one, give me the nectar of your lips; revive this servant, whose mind is fixed on you, whose body is burned by the fire of separation, and who is as good as dead.
Key message: The height of love is "I am yours" — the spirit of service (dāsya).

6. shashimukhi mukharaya maNirashanAguNam anuguNa kaNThaninAdam…
Meaning: Moon-faced one, make your jewelled girdle ring in harmony with your voice, and soothe the long torment of my ears, which have been tortured by the cuckoo's call.
Key message: True love longs even for the smallest sound of the beloved.

7. mAmapi viphalaruSA vikalIkrtam avalOkitam adhunEdam…
Meaning: You look at me, distraught by your needless anger, with eyes now half-closed in shyness. Give up the pain of love; end your anger.
Key message: The victory of love is when both together defeat anger.

8. shrI jayadEva bhaNitam idam anupada nigadita madhuripumOdam…
Meaning: May this song of Śrī Jayadeva, which tells at every step of Madhuripu's delight, increase in sensitive hearts the joy of the charming mood of love.
Key message: A heart that tastes divine love tastes the joy of the Lord Himself.

@@ 23.3 | Ślokam |
Summary
These six verses describe the union of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa in the tradition of classical śṛṅgāra poetry: an intimacy so complete that even thrill, blinking and speech seem like interruptions; Kṛṣṇa's perfect contentment, bound by Rādhā's love; Rādhā, setting out to win, herself overwhelmed; her appearance with dishevelled hair and ornaments; her face lost in bliss; and at dawn, the signs of love binding Kṛṣṇa's heart to her once again.

Upanyasam take-away
In the tradition these verses are enjoyed as a symbol of the union of the soul and the Supreme. Union of bodies is not the peak; the true union is when mind, glance, word and love all become one. The third verse holds a deep truth — Rādhā, who set out to "win", is herself overcome; at the height of love, victory and defeat, and the very sense of "I", dissolve.
Separation deepens love; deep love makes the union infinitely precious. In the last verse, the Lord's heart too remains bound to His devotee.

Detailed meaning — verse by verse

1. pratyUha: puLakAnkurENa niviDAshlESE nimESENa ca…
Meaning: In their close embrace, even the thrill of their skin was an obstacle; in their playful gazing, even the blink of an eye; in drinking the nectar of lips, even sweet talk; in their bliss, even the arts of love. Thus the union that began between them blossomed into supreme joy.
Key message: True union is the moment when mind, glance and word all become one.

2. dOrbhyAm samyamita: payOdhara bharENApIDita: pANijai:…
Meaning: Held by her arms, pressed by her embrace, marked by her nails and teeth — bound thus by Rādhā's love, her beloved attained a rare contentment. Ah, how wondrous are the ways of Love!
Key message: Only the devotee's love can bind the Lord.

3. mArAnkE ratikELi sankularaNArambhE tayA sAhasa-…
Meaning: At the start of love's battle, Rādhā boldly advanced, eager to win over her beloved; but soon her body grew still, her arms slackened, her breast trembled and her eyes closed. How can women attain valour in this battle?
Key message: At the height of love, victory and defeat and the sense of "I" dissolve.

4. vyAlOla: kEshapAsha: taraLitam aLakai: svEdalOlau kapOlau…
Meaning: Her braid loosened, her locks stirring, her cheeks bright with perspiration, the glow of her lips faded, her pearl necklace paled by the radiance of her breast, her girdle displaced — covering herself with her hands and looking at me shyly, this beauty with her tumbled garland still delights me.
Key message: Love does not merely adorn us; it transforms us.

5. ISanmIlita drSTi mugdhahasitam sItkAra dhArAvashAt…
Meaning: Eyes half-closed, a sweet smile, soft sounds of rapture, indistinct sweet words, lips lit by the gleam of her teeth — blessed is the one, lost in bliss, who drinks in the face of such a doe-eyed beauty.
Key message: The greatest treasure in the world is our place in the heart of one who loves us completely.

6. tasyA: pATala pANi jAnkitamurO nidrAkaSAyE drshau…
Meaning: At dawn — her breast marked by nails, her eyes reddened by sleep, the redness of her lips faded, her hair loose with its garland slipping, her girdle slightly loosened — these signs of Rādhā became Love's arrows and wondrously bound her beloved's heart to her.
Key message: The Lord's heart too remains bound to His devotee.

@@ 24.1 | Ślokam |
Summary
Then Rādhā — who by her love has made her beloved subject to her (svādhīnabhartṛkā) — wishing to be adorned by Him, speaks to her lover Mādhava, who is weary from love's play.

Upanyasam take-away
At the start of the Gīta Govindam Rādhā wandered searching for Kṛṣṇa; now she is "svādhīnabhartṛkā" — the heroine whose beloved is under her sway. A heart won by love is deeper than a heart won by power. The Lord who rules the universe yields only to His devotee's love.

Detailed meaning

1. atha kAntam ratishrAntam abhimaNDana vAnchayA…
Meaning: Then the lovely Rādhā, who had made her beloved her own, wishing to be adorned, spoke to Mādhava, her lover, who was weary from love.
Key message: Even the Lord of the universe yields to His devotee's love.

@@ 24.2 | Aṣṭapadi 24 — Kuru Yadunandana |
Summary
The final aṣṭapadi. Rādhā asks the son of the Yadus for one thing after another: "With your hand, cooler than sandal, draw a musk design on my breast; restore the kohl on my eyes; place earrings on my ears; arrange my hair before my face; put a musk tilaka on my forehead; set flowers and peacock feathers in my hair; put the girdle and garment on my waist." So she spoke to the son of Nanda, who delights the heart and is like sandal to His devotees.

Upanyasam take-away
"Nijagāda sā yadunandane krīḍati hṛdayānandane nandanandane bhaktacandane" — the son of Nanda, who is cooling sandal to His devotees. The journey was all burning; at its end comes coolness.
There is a beautiful reversal here: in the previous aṣṭapadi Kṛṣṇa pressed Rādhā's feet; now Rādhā, with full claim, says "adorn me." True surrender is this — offering oneself to the Lord, saying "You adorn my life." Adorning the soul is the Lord's work; the soul's part is only to give itself to Him.
The last stanza is the phalaśruti of the whole Gīta Govindam — "haricaraṇa smaraṇāmṛta nirmita kalikaluṣa jvara khaṇḍane": this poem, made of the nectar of remembering Hari's feet, is the remedy for the fever of the sins of the Kali age.

Detailed meaning — stanza by stanza

1. kuru yadunandana candana shishira tarENa karENa payOdharE…
Meaning: "Son of the Yadus, with your hand cooler than sandal, draw a musk design on my breast, sister to Love's auspicious pitcher" — so she spoke to the son of Nanda, who delights the heart and is like sandal to His devotees.
Key message: Offering oneself to the Lord, saying "You adorn my life," is surrender.

2. alikulabhanjanam anjanakam ratinAyaka sAyaka mOcanE…
Meaning: Beloved, make the kohl, darker than a swarm of bees and smudged by the kisses of your lips, shine again on my eyes, which loose arrows like the god of Love.
Key message: Where there is love, beauty is no longer outer decoration but a sign of love.

3. nayana kuranga taranga vikAsa nirAsakarE shrutimaNDalE…
Meaning: On my ears, which set the limit to the leaping of the deer of my eyes, place earrings like Love's noose.
Key message: In love one does not change the other but completes their beauty.

4. bhramaracayam racayantam upari ruciram suciram mama sammukhE…
Meaning: Before my face, which surpasses the lotus in purity, arrange playfully the lovely locks like a swarm of bees, so that they stay a long time.
Key message: Beauty is completed only by the love that cherishes it.

5. mrgamada rasavalitam lalitam kuru tilakam alika rajanIkarE…
Meaning: Lotus-faced one, on the moon of my forehead, with its mark-like spot and drops of perspiration, place a lovely tilaka of musk.
Key message: Even weariness experienced with love becomes a sweet memory.

6. ghanarucirE cikurE kuru mAnada mAnasajadhvaja cAmarE…
Meaning: Giver of honour, in my hair — lovely as a cloud, like a whisk on Love's banner, tousled by play — set flowers and peacock feathers.
Key message: Love turns even life's disorder into beautiful memories.

7. sarasaghanE jaghanE mama shambara dAraNa vAraNa kandarE…
Meaning: Handsome one, on my hips — like a cave for the elephant of Love, the slayer of Śambara — put on the jewelled girdle, the garment and the ornaments.
Key message: Great love shows itself in the small care that follows.

8. shrI jayadEva ruciravacanE hrdayam sadayam kurumaNDanE…
Meaning: Adorner, set your heart kindly upon these lovely words of Śrī Jayadeva, made of the nectar of remembering Hari's feet, which destroy the fever of the sins of the Kali age.
Key message: When the summit of śṛṅgāra becomes devotion, rasa becomes spiritual experience.

@@ 24.3 | Ślokam — Conclusion of the twelfth sarga and of the poem |
Summary
Six verses conclude the twelfth sarga, "Suprīta Pītāmbara", and the entire Gīta Govinda mahākāvya. Pītāmbara adorns Rādhā just as she asked. Then comes the blessing: may Viṣṇu, reclining on Ādiśeṣa and reflected as many forms in the serpent's jewels, protect us. May Hari, recalling how Lakṣmī chose Him at her svayaṃvara on the shore of the milk ocean, protect us. Finally Jayadeva states the excellence of his poem and the fruit of singing it.

Upanyasam take-away
The whole journey of the Gīta Govindam in one line: meeting → separation → longing → message → intense separation → going to meet → delay → anger → reconciliation → union → play. It is not only the story of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa; it is the soul's journey to the Supreme.
The second verse gives a beautiful close: the cowherd boy of the Yamunā bank is finally seen as Śrīman Nārāyaṇa reclining on Ādiśeṣa. A journey that began through human love ends in the vision of the Supreme.
The last verse: honey, sugar, grapes, nectar, milk — none equals the sweetness of Jayadeva's words. True rasa is not tasted by the tongue but experienced by the heart. As long as these words, the essence of śṛṅgāra, endure, remembrance of Hari will endure in the world.

Detailed meaning — verse by verse

1. racaya kucayO: patram citram kuruSva kapOlayO:…
Meaning: "Draw leaf designs on my breast; decorate my cheeks; place the girdle on my hips; set a lovely garland in my hair; put bangles on my hands and anklets on my feet" — told this, Pītāmbara, delighted, did exactly so.
Key message: At the height of love service flows both ways — the Lord Himself serves the devotee.

2. paryankIkrta nAganAyaka phaNAshrENI maNInAm gaNE…
Meaning: May Hari protect us — who, reflected in the gems on the hoods of the serpent-king He has made His couch, appears in many forms, as if He had taken on many bodies with a thousand eyes, wishing to behold Lakṣmī, daughter of the ocean, who holds His lotus feet.
Key message: The cowherd of the Yamunā bank is Śrīman Nārāyaṇa reclining on Śeṣa.

3. tvAm aprApya mayi svayamvaraparAm kSIrOda tIrOdarE…
Meaning: "Beautiful one, on the shore of the milk ocean, failing to win you — who chose me at your svayaṃvara — I suppose foolish Śiva, lord of Pārvatī, drank the poison!" Turning Lakṣmī's mind with such old tales and casting His glance upon her bosom, may Hari protect us.
Key message: Love and humour mingle in the Lord's play; Rādhā and Lakṣmī are the same principle of Śrī.

4. yadgAndharva kalAsu kaushalam anudhyAnam ca yadvaiSNavam…
Meaning: Whatever skill there is in the art of music, whatever meditation on Viṣṇu, whatever discernment of the truth of śṛṅgāra, whatever grace in poetry — let the wise joyfully learn all of it from this Śrī Gīta Govindam of the learned poet Jayadeva, whose mind was one with Kṛṣṇa.
Key message: True art leads us from the artist to the Supreme.

5. yannityai: vacanai: virinci girijA prANEshamukhyai: muhu:…
Meaning: That primal reality, which even the eternal words of Brahmā, Śiva and others, and those skilled in the essence of many inquiries, have not yet fully determined — may it, set forth in Jayadeva's poem and refined by good sayings, shine in the hearts of those who have reached the limit of essence.
Key message: Knowing the Supreme needs the intellect; experiencing Him needs the heart.

6. sAdhvI mAdhvIka cintA na bhavati bhavata: sharkarE karkashA'si…
Meaning: Honey, even the thought of you is no longer pleasing. Sugar, you are coarse. Grapes, who will look at you? Nectar, you are as good as dead. Milk, your taste is mere water. Mango, weep. Beloved's lip, sink into the earth. For as long as Jayadeva's words, the essence of śṛṅgāra, spread everywhere (none of you can equal them).
Key message: True rasa is not tasted by the tongue but experienced by the heart. Here ends the Śrī Gīta Govinda mahākāvya.
