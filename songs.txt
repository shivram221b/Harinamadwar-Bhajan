## 0 | About the book
@@ i | Introduction |  | 1
In this world full of desires, distractions, and duties, we are all searching for peace, for joy, for love that doesn’t fade. The great saints of India discovered a secret: what we seek is not far; it is within us, and it awakens through devotion.

In the Bhagavad Gita, Lord Krishna describes four types of devotees:
1. Aartha Bhakta – who comes to God in times of suffering.
2. Arthaarthee Bhakta – who seeks worldly benefits.
3. Jignaasu Bhakta – who is curious to understand the Divine.
4. Jnaani Bhakta – who desires nothing but God Himself.

While each one is accepted with compassion, the one who loves God for His sake alone with no expectation is the dearest to Him. This is Prema Bhakti. Not just a feeling, but a way of being. Not something we do, but something we become.

Prahlada’s Timeless Teaching
Even as a young boy, Prahlada lived this truth. When asked what he had learned, he said:

śravaṇaṁ kīrtanaṁ viṣṇoḥ smaraṇaṁ pāda-sevanam |
arcanaṁ vandanaṁ dāsyaṁ sakhyam ātma-nivedanam ||

Meaning:
Devotion (bhakthi) is listening to the glories of the Lord (śravaṇam), singing His names (kīrtanam), remembering Him (smaraṇam), serving His feet (pāda- sevanam), offering worship (arcanam), prostrating (vandanam), living as His servant (dāsyam), befriending Him (sakhyam), and surrendering one’s whole self (ātma-nivedanam).

The Way of the Saints
We listen to the songs of saints who lived these paths. Saints like:

- Bhadrachala Ramadasar, who found freedom even in a prison cell
-  Purandara Dasar, who gave voice to devotion in every home
-  Sadasiva Brahmendra, who walked the earth in silence and song
-  Thyagaraja, whose music was soaked in Rama Bhakti
-  Annamacharya, the poet of Tirumala
-  Narayana Theerthar, who brought Krishna’s leelas to the stage
-  Vijaya Gopalar, the jewel of Divyanama sampradaya
-  Meera Bai, whose soul danced for Krishna
-  Tulsidas, who breathed Rama
-  The Alwars, whose Tamil verses still stir the soul
-  The Marathi saints, who lifted villages into devotion with abhangs

These were not mere composers. They were lovers of God, each expressing Nava Vidha Bhakti in their own way especially through Nama Sankeerthanam.

Why Namasankeerthanam?
Namasankeerthanam singing the Lord’s name is not a performance. It is not about musical skill. It is about melting the ego, and allowing the heart to speak.

The Lord once told Mahalakshmi:

“There are only two simple ways to free a soul from samsara:
- Namasankeerthanam (singing the Divine Name)
-  Prapatti (complete surrender).
Even if we cannot do complex rituals, even if our minds wander in meditation, we can call out the Name. Let it come from the tongue, it will reach the heart.

What Is “Ananda”?
Ananda is not just happiness.
Happiness comes and goes.
Ananda is a state. It is peace that stays, even when the world changes. It is what the saints lived in. It is what Prema Bhakti leads to.

It is the feeling of fullness, of being held, of knowing: “I am not alone. I belong to the Divine.”

The various kirtans that we listen and learn is a lamp for those walking the path of bhakti. It is not just about learning songs; it is about remembering who we are. Bhakti is not something you do once a week. It is who you are becoming, moment by moment.

When we sing together, we grow together. When we remember His Name, we begin to forget our ego.

The Role of Harinamadwar
To support this sacred journey, a loving space has been created “Harinamadwar”

It is a humble gateway for like-minded seekers, a place to: - Gather in satsang, Learn and sing together, Share the power of Nama,And grow in devotion.

Harinamadwar is not just a group. It is a spiritual movement. A community that believes Bhakti is a way of life and Nama is the path that makes it sweet, simple, and strong. Together, through Harinamadwar, we can spread this light to more hearts. So that Nama doesn’t remain in books or bhajan halls, But flows in our homes, our families, and our breath every single day.

Let us walk the path of the saints.
Let us celebrate the Name.
Let us live in Prema.
Let us rest in Ananda.

Why learn Divyanama Sankeerthanam Pantati?
Divyanama Sankeerthanam is not just a musical tradition — it is a sacred journey through the hearts of great saints who poured their deepest devotion into song. The Pantati, or structured sequence, is a carefully preserved pathway that helps us relive the divine emotions experienced by our Gurus — from Narayana Theerthar to Thyagaraja, from Meerabai to Purandara Dasa.

Each song in the Pantati is chosen not just for its lyrical beauty, but for its spiritual purpose. When we sing in this sacred order, we are not merely performing; we are immersing ourselves in a divine experience — reliving the bhakti bhava, yearning, surrender, and joy that flowed from the saints’ hearts.

Radha Kalyanam and Divyanama Sankeerthanam transport us to a realm where the divine play of Radha and Krishna becomes personal, intimate, and transformative. Learning this tradition is not just about knowing the songs — it is about awakening the soul.

In every note, we echo a saint’s longing. In every step, we walk a time- honored path to the Divine.

1. We begin with the Dhyana Slokam and Thodayamangalam

3. After the Ashtapadis, we proceed to Panchapati, which consists of compositions by the following saints/gurus, in this order:
• Tharangam – Narayana Theerthar
• Bhadrachala Ramadasa Keerthana

• Gopala Krishna Bharathi

@@ iii | Radha kalyana pantati |  | 6
About Radha Kalyanam pantati

Bhagavan Sri Krishna emphatically told Naradha that he always resided with his devotees performing Namasankeerthanam. It may be noted that namasankeerthanam is an easy sadana available in kaliyuga to reach god as other ways and means like doing yagna and yaagas are difficult / cumbersome. Namasankeerthanam does not require anything else except pure devotion towards Him. Hence it has been recognized as the purest, easiest and surest path to reach God for any aspirant irrespective of caste or creed. All our religious scripts and hymns direct every one of us towards this path only.

Radha Kalyanam is considered to be the Jeeva Brahma Ikyam. The Jeevaatma merging with Pasramatma. Radha is the embodiment of Bhakthi and Madhava the Purusha (Brahman). A Bhaktha rejoining with Brahman is the spiritual essence of Radha Kalyanam. However, it is being performed in the normal way like other Kalyana Utsavams for our own convenience and enjoyment externally.

How to understand about formalities Kotnotsavam (Muthukkuthal), Varaprekshana, Chhornika, Pravaram, Kanyaka Dhanam, Panigrahanam and Thirumangalya Dharanam etc. etc.?

Context / background
Sri Nandagopal has gone to the forest for cattle feeding and took the child Krishna with him on his shoulders. Suddenly the sky went dark and thunder and lightning started and there was a heavy downpour of rain. He lamented that Krishna would get frightened and should be sent home and was looking around, when he found Radha Devi coming along. He called out for Radha and gave the child to her and asked her to fetch him home. She held the child lovingly close to her and was walking. In fact this climatic change was brought about by Krishna for getting such a chance to make Radha realize her original form. Suddenly the Goloka scene was created with high buildings and Upaarikai and Madhava and Radha rejoicing in their original form. Seeing this, Radha Devi suddenly realized her original status and tried to rush to Krishna with both hands spread apart in joy forgetting about that she was holding the child. At that time, the child disappeared and Lord Madhava appeared in her presence. As Madhava and Radha are in human form of Krishna and Radha, Krishna felt they should abide by the rules and hence Krishna called Brahma Deva and asked him to perform the marriage immediately as per vedic rites and Brahma Deva performed it with all formalities like Pravaram, Kanyaka Dhanam, Panigrahanam and Homams. This is the meaning of the Choornikai in short. Thus, there is a proof that the Kalyanam was performed in full form like any other Kalyana Utsavam. Hence, there is nothing wrong in performing it according to the Padhathi given to us by the Bhajan Gurus.

Kotnotsavam (Muthu kutthal)
In Telugu Sampradayam, paddy (rice covered with a light skin) is poured into a wooden Stand with hollow side on top and a round flat base) and it is crushed with ULAKKAI (a long round shaped thick stick) decorated with Mango Leaves, red and white colour lines, flower etc. The powder coming out of this crushing is taken and poured into the cloth around the couples’ waiste and later they are taken for Oil both, to be brushed with this powder. Then they are offered dresses by the Priest which they wear in the marriage. As the Padhathi is given by Telugu Gurus, they have prescribed the same method. Here we assume that Muthu, Vairam, Vaidooryam etc. are poured into the Ural (nowadays represented by Akshathai – rice mixed in Haldi) and crushed with Ulakkai to make it a fine powder which is poured imaginarily into the cloth around the waiste of the couple and later they are bathed with fragrant oil (known as Thanadu Shobanai) and offered dresses. This is called Muthukkuthal and Thalandu Shobhanai. These are performed with special songs, describing them.

Here, one should look into the spiritual importance. The Ural is compared with our Pure Mind, the Ulakkai with Omakara Naadam and the Akshathai with us as Jeevaatmaan – the Satwic natured Jeeva (white coloured rice) encompassed by Maaya (the skin covering the rice). For realization of God, we need to keep our mind pure, free from the Kama Krodhaadhi Shatgunas, away from Thripudi etc. into which pour ourselves – the jeevaatmaas (in meditation).

For meditation, as the mind needs the co-operation of the Panchendriyas (which bring the stimuli from outside into our mind) five Kanyakas (girls below the age of 11 years, who are considered to be free from Kalmasham (evil thoughts)) representing the Five Sense Organs are made to pour these Akshathai (Jeevatma) into the Ural (Mind), and five Bhagavathaas (representing the five matured Karmendriyas) repeatedly hit the rice in the Ural with the Ulakkai (Omkara Naadam,) to the tune of songs describing the qualities of the Lord.(known as Muthukkuthal) and remove the covering (Maaya) – known as Muthukkozhithal – and bring out the white coloured akshathai (Jeevatma) and pour it uno the roshtrum of the Lord. This is the symbolization of Jeevatma, released from Maaya and having become pure, merges into the Brahman.

The entire process of Muthukkuthal and Muthukkozhithal (Kotnotsavam) represents the process of meditation, which is done with the co-operation of Five Sense Organs and Five Karmendriyas and chanting inside the Omkara Naadam as described above. Thus, this is a process of realization of God through meditation. If we have this mindset while performing the Kalyanam, that is more than enough. We will be blessed with Lord;s grace. To commemorate the Jeeva Brahma Ikyam as above, whereby the Bhaktha becomes Jeevan Muktha. Harathi is taken

Musala Narthanam
Bhagawathas dance with joy with the Ulakkai on hand to the tune of the Ashtapathi song. This depicts the joy of Jeevan Muktha, who dances in joy as he stays at this state for ever. Also, once the Jeevaatma merges with Paramatma, there is no question of Dwandhwa Bhava and hence Omkaram does not exist separately. The Brahman, Omkara and Jeeva all are one now. Hereafter the Jeevan Muktha ever shines joyous in the Blissful status.
